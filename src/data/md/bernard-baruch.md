# Bernard Baruch — The Lone Wolf of Wall Street Who Sold Before the Crash

## 1. THE HOOK

In 1929, the stock market crashed. Most investors lost everything. Bernard Baruch did not. He had sold his stocks before the crash. He preserved his wealth. He was called "The Lone Wolf of Wall Street." He made his fortune in the market, lost most of it, made it back, and then got out before the crash. He advised presidents—Wilson, FDR, Truman, Eisenhower. He gave advice while sitting on park benches across from the White House. He was called a "park bench statesman." He lived from 1870 to 1965. He saw the Gilded Age, the Roaring Twenties, the Great Depression, and two World Wars. He made money, lost money, made it back, and preserved it.

How does a man who lived through the worst crashes in history preserve his wealth?

## 2. ORIGIN — The "Before" (The Relatability Engine)

Bernard Baruch was born August 19, 1870, in South Carolina. His family moved to New York. He studied at the City College of New York. He did not go to business school. He learned finance on Wall Street.

He was running from poverty. His family was not wealthy. He wanted financial security.

## 3. THE FIRE — Failures, Rock-Bottom & Turning Points

### Making a Fortune

In the late 1800s and early 1900s, Baruch made a fortune in the stock market. He was a speculator. He bought and sold stocks. He used leverage. He made money.

### Losing It

In the 1920s, Baruch lost most of his fortune. He had speculated. The market turned. He lost. He nearly went broke.

### Making It Back

Baruch rebuilt his fortune. He speculated again. He was disciplined. He made his money back.

### The 1929 Crash

In 1929, Baruch sold his stocks. He saw speculation. He saw high prices. He got out. When the market crashed, he preserved his wealth. His famous quote: "I made my money by selling too soon."

### Advising Presidents

Baruch advised presidents—Wilson, FDR, Truman, Eisenhower. He was called a "park bench statesman" because he gave advice while sitting on benches in Lafayette Park across from the White House.

## 4. THE GRIND — The Invisible Years (The Method)

Baruch's method is defined by discipline, cutting losses, and contrarianism.

### Discipline

Baruch was disciplined. He cut losses. He did not let losing positions ride. When he was wrong, he exited.

### Contrarianism

Baruch sold when others were buying. In 1929, he sold too soon. He did not wait for the top. He got out.

### Public Service

After making his fortune, Baruch focused on public service. He advised presidents. He worked on war mobilization. He was involved in policy.

The invisible years—the years before 1929—were years of making, losing, and remaking fortunes. Baruch learned from losses. He did not repeat mistakes.

## 5. THE TEST — The Breakthrough Moment

The 1929 crash was the test. Baruch sold before the crash. He preserved his wealth. The test was not making money. The test was preserving it.

## 7. THE LEGACY — Purpose Beyond Profit + Human Side

Bernard Baruch died in June 1965. He lived long enough to see the market crash, the Great Depression, World War II, and the postwar boom.

### Public Service

Baruch advised presidents. He was involved in war mobilization. He worked on international policy.

### What does he regret?

Baruch lost most of his fortune in the 1920s before remaking it. He learned from the loss.

### What does he want to be remembered for?

In his autobiography, Baruch emphasized public service. He wanted to be remembered for his advice to presidents and his contributions to war mobilization.

## 8. FINAL MOTIVATION & PERSONAL APPLICATION Guide

Bernard Baruch's career demonstrates that preserving wealth matters more than making it.

3. **Be disciplined** — Baruch was a speculator. But he was disciplined. He did not let losing positions ride.

4. **Don't be afraid to be wrong** — "The main thing in life is not to be afraid to be wrong." Baruch made mistakes. He learned.

5. **Preserve wealth** — Making money is not enough. You must preserve it. Baruch sold before the 1929 crash. He preserved.

The key is not to make money. The key is to preserve it.
