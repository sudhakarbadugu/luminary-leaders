# Richard Dennis — The Prince of the Pit Who Proved Trading Can Be Taught

## 1. THE HOOK

In 1983, he made a bet with his partner. Richard Dennis said: trading can be taught. His partner William Eckhardt said: trading is innate—it cannot be taught. To prove his point, Dennis recruited 23 people from diverse backgrounds. Some were poker players. Some were security guards. Some had no financial experience at all. He trained them for two weeks. Then he gave them money to trade. Many of them became successful traders. The experiment proved that trading can be taught. The traders were called "Turtles"—because Dennis said they grow like turtles. He turned $1,600 borrowed money into an estimated $200 million fortune. Then he lost it in 1987 and again in 1998. His career demonstrates that even the best traders face drawdowns. His experiment demonstrates that trading can be taught.

How does a trader prove that trading can be taught?

## 2. ORIGIN — The "Before" (The Relatability Engine)

Richard Dennis was born in January 1949 in Chicago, Illinois. He grew up in Chicago, the center of commodities trading.

He started trading with $1,600 borrowed money. He was in his early twenties. He traded at the Chicago Board of Trade. In the 1970s, he bought soybean contracts at historic lows. The prices went up. He made millions. He became known as the "Prince of the Pit."

He was running from the conventional path. He did not go to business school. He did not work at an investment bank. He went to the trading floor.

## 3. THE FIRE — Failures, Rock-Bottom & Turning Points

### The Soybean Trade

In the 1970s, Dennis bought soybean contracts at historic lows. The prices went up. He made millions. The trade established his reputation.

### The Turtle Traders Experiment (1983)

In 1983, Dennis made a bet with his partner William Eckhardt. Dennis believed that trading could be taught. Eckhardt believed that trading was innate. To settle the bet, Dennis recruited 23 people from diverse backgrounds. He trained them for two weeks. He taught them his trend-following system. Then he gave them money to trade.

Many of the Turtles became successful. Some earned millions. The experiment proved that trading can be taught. Dennis won the bet.

### The 1987 Crash

In 1987, the market crashed. Dennis lost money. The losses were significant. He faced criticism. Some said that his system had stopped working.

### The 1998 Russian Debt Crisis

In 1998, the Russian debt default caused losses in emerging markets. Dennis lost money again. After these setbacks, he retired from active trading.

## 4. THE GRIND — The Invisible Years (The Method)

Dennis's method is defined by trend following, systematic rules, and discipline.

### Trend Following

Dennis did not predict markets. He followed trends. He bought when prices went up. He sold when prices went down. The method is not complex. The execution is.

### Systematic Rules

Dennis had rules. The rules defined when to buy, when to sell, and how to size positions. The Turtles were taught the same rules. The rules did not require genius. They required discipline.

### Discipline

Dennis's famous quote: "I could publish my trading rules in the newspaper and no one would follow them." The rules are not the secret. The secret is following them.

The invisible years—the years of trading before the Turtles—were years of testing and refining the system. Dennis did not invent trend following. He executed it with discipline.

## 5. THE TEST — The Breakthrough Moment

The Turtle Traders experiment was the test. Dennis recruited people with no trading experience. He taught them his system. Many became successful. The test proved that trading can be taught.

## 6. THE PHILOSOPHY — Stealable Wisdom

**On Teaching:**
Trading can be taught. The Turtles proved it. You do not need innate talent. You need a system and discipline.

**On Discipline:**
"I could publish my trading rules in the newspaper and no one would follow them." Knowing the rules is not enough. Following them is the hard part.

**On Mistakes:**
"Trading is even more about not making mistakes than about making good decisions." The key is not to be brilliant. The key is to avoid mistakes.

## 7. THE LEGACY — Purpose Beyond Profit + Human Side

Richard Dennis retired from trading after the 1998 losses. He remains one of the most influential traders in history.

### What does he regret?

Dennis has said that the 1987 and 1998 losses were difficult. He retired from active trading.

### What does he want to be remembered for?

In interviews, Dennis emphasizes the Turtle Traders experiment. He proved that trading can be taught.

## 8. FINAL MOTIVATION & PERSONAL APPLICATION Guide

Richard Dennis's career demonstrates that trading is teachable and that even the best traders face drawdowns.

**5 Actionable Steps This Week:**

1. **Trading can be taught** — If you think you need innate talent, you are wrong. You need a system and discipline.

2. **Follow the rules** — Dennis said the rules could be published and no one would follow them. The difficulty is not in the rules. It is in following them.

3. **Avoid mistakes** — "Trading is even more about not making mistakes than about making good decisions." Cut losses. Follow position sizing. Avoid catastrophic errors.

4. **Expect drawdowns** — Even Dennis, one of the greatest traders, faced losses in 1987 and 1998. Drawdowns are inevitable. Survive them.

5. **Prove it works** — The Turtles proved that trading can be taught. If you are skeptical, test a system. See if it works.

**The Hard Question:**

If Dennis published his rules and no one followed them—what rules do you know but not follow?

The secret is not the rules. The secret is the discipline.
