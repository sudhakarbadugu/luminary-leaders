# Babar Azam — The Prince of Pakistan's Batters

## 1. THE HOOK

He was born into a cricket-playing family in Lahore, overshadowed in his early years by cousins who had already played for Pakistan. But by his mid-20s, Babar Azam had broken records for the fastest to 5,000, 6,000, and 7,000 ODI runs, become the top-ranked T20I batsman in the world, and been appointed captain of Pakistan in all formats. His elegant cover drives and composed demeanor drew comparisons to Virat Kohli — and he was still improving.

How does a boy from Lahore become the most technically refined batsman of his generation in a country where inconsistency had long plagued the batting order?

## 2. ORIGIN — The "Before" (The Relatability Engine)

Babar Azam was born October 15, 1994, in Lahore, Pakistan, into a family where cricket was already present. His cousins — Kamran Akmal, Umar Akmal, and Adnan Akmal — had played international cricket for Pakistan. His uncle was involved in club cricket. The family background was working-middle class; his father ran a small business. Lahore is a cricket-crazed city in Punjab, but Babar was not the player everyone talked about from a young age.

Growing up, he watched his cousins play on the international stage. He practiced. He attended the same academies. But he was small for his age, quiet, introverted. He did not have the presence of a future star.

He worked his way through age-group cricket. In Pakistan's cricket system, age-group performances often do not translate directly to international success. Babar was not a prodigy like India's Sachin Tendulkar or Sri Lanka's Arjuna Ranatunga. He was a consistent scorer, a technically sound player, who needed time to show what he could become.

He was running from the expectation that a quiet boy from Lahore would disappear into domestic cricket, never heard from again on the international stage. He wanted to prove that consistency could be as valuable as explosive talent.

## 3. THE FIRE — Failures, Rock-Bottom & Turning Points

### The Comparison

From his earliest international appearances, Babar was compared to Virat Kohli. They had similar batting styles — right-handed, top-order, elegant cover drives, reliance on timing more than power. Both were calm under pressure. Both wore their team's batting hopes on their shoulders.

The comparison was flattering but suffocating. Kohli had already established himself as one of the greatest batsmen of his era. Babar had not. He was compared to Kohli before he had played enough matches to have a career.

### The Strike Rate Criticism

In his early years, Babar was criticized for slow strike rates, especially in T20 cricket. Pakistan's fans expected aggression. Babar played classical cricket: he took his time, built the innings, accelerated later. In an era of power-hitting, this was seen as outdated.

The criticism cut deep. Fans on social media compared him unfavorably to other Pakistani batsmen who played more aggressively. His T20 strike rate lingered around 120 — good for Tests and ODIs, but low for T20s.

Babar adjusted. He worked on his power hitting. He improved his sweep shots. He learned to attack earlier. His strike rates improved. By 2022, he was scoring at a T20 strike rate above 130, while maintaining his classical technique.

### The Captaincy Pressure

Babar was appointed Pakistan's white-ball captain in 2019 and Test captain in 2020. Leading Pakistan is one of the most high-pressure jobs in world cricket. The fans are passionate, the media is critical, and the political and administrative tensions within the Pakistan Cricket Board (PCB) are constant.

Under his captaincy, Pakistan reached the semi-finals of the 2021 T20 World Cup and the final of the 2022 T20 World Cup. But the team's losses — particularly the loss to Zimbabwe in the 2023 ODI World Cup — led to intense criticism.

In late 2023 and 2024, Babar stepped down from captaincy in all formats following pressure from the PCB, media, and fans. The decision was controversial. Some analysts argued that removing the captain was a mistake; others argued that Babar was not suited for leadership. The episode exposed the volatility of professional cricket in Pakistan.

## 4. THE GRIND — The Invisible Years (The Method)

Babar's method is characterized by classical batting applied to modern formats.

### Technical Foundation

His batting is built on a solid defense, excellent footwork, and a wide range of scoring shots. His cover drive is considered one of the best in the game. Unlike many modern players who rely on power, Babar relies on timing.

He learned this method by practicing for hours — net sessions, shadow batting, video analysis. He studied the great batsmen: Sachin Tendulkar, Ricky Ponting, and of course, Virat Kohli.

### Fitness

Babar has maintained high fitness standards throughout his career. He is not the most physically imposing player, but he has endurance and agility that allow him to run hard between wickets and field well. In Pakistani cricket, where fitness has historically been a weakness, Babar has been a model.

### Adaptation

The improvement in his T20 strike rate is the result of deliberate work. He identified a weakness, practiced the specific shots he needed, and changed his approach without sacrificing his core technique.

### Leadership

As captain, Babar led by example rather than by force. He was not a vocal, aggressive captain. He was calm, supportive, and led with his batting. This style worked in some contexts and was criticized in others.

The invisible years — the years of practice, the years of criticism, the years of adjustment — made him the player who could score consistently across formats and conditions.

## 5. THE TEST — The Breakthrough Moment

Babar's breakthrough was not a single innings but a series of record-breaking achievements.

### Record-Breaking ODI Milestones

- Fastest to 5,000 ODI runs (97 innings, tied with Hashim Amla)
- Fastest to 6,000 ODI runs (114 innings, breaking Hashim Amla's record)
- Fastest to 7,000 ODI runs (143 innings)
- Fastest to 8,000 ODI runs (163 innings)

These records demonstrate consistency across years. A player who scores quickly occasionally can set a high average briefly. A player who scores quickly over years is rare.

### Key Innings

- 196 vs Australia, Karachi 2022: On a difficult pitch, in the fourth innings of a Test, Babar scored a century that nearly secured an unlikely victory. Pakistan lost, but Babar's innings was praised as one of the best by a Pakistani batsman in recent years.
- 158 vs England, Birmingham 2021: In an ODI, Babar scored 158 off 139 balls. It demonstrated that his classical technique could produce modern, aggressive innings.

### T20 World Cup 2022 Final

Pakistan reached the final of the 2022 T20 World Cup. Babar had been criticized throughout the tournament for slow starts. In the final, England set Pakistan a target of 138. Pakistan's chase was difficult. Babar was dismissed for 32. Pakistan lost. The criticism resumed.

## 6. THE PHILOSOPHY — Stealable Wisdom

**On Technique:**
Babar has repeatedly said that his classical technique is not a limitation but a foundation. In an era of power hitting, he has scored runs by playing proper cricket shots. His argument: if your basics are sound, you can adapt to any format.

**On Patience:**
He has been criticized for slow strike rates, especially early in his innings. His response has been to stay patient and trust that runs will come. Not every ball can be hit for six. Not every over needs to yield double-digit runs.

**On Criticism:**
Babar has faced criticism from media, fans, and former players throughout his career. He has consistently responded with performance. In interviews, he says that criticism is part of playing for Pakistan, and that his job is to perform on the field.

**Key Quotes (reported):**
- "I believe in my ability and preparation. I know my game."
- "Pressure is part of cricket. You have to handle it."

## 7. THE LEGACY — Purpose Beyond Profit + Human Side

Babar Azam is still playing (as of 2024). He resigned from captaincy to focus on his batting. His legacy is still being written.

What is clear thus far:

- He is one of the most technically correct batsmen of his generation
- He holds records for consistency in ODI cricket
- His elegance has drawn comparisons to Virat Kohli
- He led Pakistan to a T20 World Cup final
- He faced and survived criticism about strike rate, captaincy, and performance

### What does he regret?

He has not spoken publicly about regrets. He has expressed disappointment about the loss in the 2022 T20 World Cup final. The captaincy pressure in Pakistan is intense, and the criticism he faced was often personal.

### What does he want to be remembered for?

In interviews, he has emphasized that he wants to win matches for Pakistan. The records, he has said, are a byproduct of trying to help his team.

## 8. FINAL MOTIVATION & PERSONAL APPLICATION GUIDE

Babar Azam's career demonstrates that classical methods, applied consistently and refined over time, can succeed in modern formats. He did not reinvent batting; he refined the existing technique. He did not change his personality to fit a captaincy role; he led by performance.

**5 Actionable Steps This Week:**

1. **Master the basics before expanding** — Babar's cover drives and defense are the foundation for his entire game. Before you develop advanced skills, ensure your fundamentals are strong.

2. **Criticize your own weaknesses before others do** — Babar was criticized for his T20 strike rate. He adjusted. If there is a weakness in your skill set that others have mentioned or that you have noticed, address it before it becomes a criticism.

3. **Let performance answer criticism** — The natural response to criticism is to argue. Babar has consistently let his batting respond. In your work, perform before you defend.

4. **Focus on timing, not power** — In any field, the loud and powerful attract attention. But the player who has timing, timing that allows them to perform consistently, often outlasts the powerful. Develop your sense of what moment is right rather than always pushing.

5. **Stay calm under pressure** — Babar's demeanor at the crease is calm. Whatever the situation — chase, collapse, or dominant position — his expression is similar. Learn to maintain composure; panic rarely helps.

**The Hard Question:**

If a boy from Lahore who was told he was too slow, too quiet, and too similar to someone else could become the fastest to 8,000 ODI runs in history — what criticism of your style are you accepting instead of using as a reason to adapt?

Your style is not your limitation. Your refusal to adapt your style is.
