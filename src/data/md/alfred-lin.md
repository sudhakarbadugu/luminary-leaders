# Alfred Lin — The Operator Who Built Zappos and Invested in Unicorns

## 1. THE HOOK

He was CFO and COO at Zappos. He worked with Tony Hsieh. Amazon acquired Zappos for $1.2 billion in 2009. He is a partner at Sequoia Capital since 2010. He invested in Airbnb, DoorDash, Stripe, Uber, Houzz, KiwiCo. He was born in Taiwan. He moved to the US as a child. He studied at Harvard and Stanford. He coined "Lin's Law": ask if spending will bring customers back. He says: "Great companies are built on repeat customers."

How does a Taiwanese immigrant become one of Sequoia's best investors?

## 2. ORIGIN — THE "Before" (The Relatability Engine)

Alfred Lin was born in 1973 or 1974 in Taiwan. He moved to the US as a child. He studied at Harvard (BA) and Stanford (MBA).

He was running from conventional careers. He saw the internet. He joined Zappos.

## 3. THE FIRE — Failures, Rock-Bottom & Turning Points

### Tellme Networks

Lin was VP at Tellme Networks. He learned voice technology.

### Zappos

Lin was CFO and COO at Zappos. He worked with Tony Hsieh. He helped build the culture. Amazon acquired it for $1.2 billion in 2009.

### Sequoia Capital (2010-Present)

In 2010, Lin joined Sequoia Capital. He became one of the best VCs of his generation.

### Investments

Lin invested in Airbnb, DoorDash, Stripe, Uber, Houzz, KiwiCo. Multiple unicorns.

## 4. THE GRIND — The Invisible Years (The Method)

Lin's method is defined by customer obsession, operational expertise, and culture.

### Lin's Law

Lin's famous test: "Every time you spend money, ask: will this bring customers back?" Focus on what matters.

### Customer Obsession

Lin's philosophy: "Great companies are built on repeat customers." Focus on retention.

### Culture

Lin's philosophy: "Culture is what happens when no one is looking." Build values.

The invisible years—the years at Zappos—taught Lin about culture and customers. He learned from Tony Hsieh.

## 5. THE TEST — The Breakthrough Moment

The investments—Airbnb, DoorDash, Stripe—are the test. Lin identified the winners. He helped them scale.

## 6. THE PHILOSOPHY — Stealable Wisdom

**On Customers:**
"Great companies are built on repeat customers."

**On Culture:**
"Culture is what happens when no one is looking."

**On Founders:**
"The best founders are obsessed with the customer experience."

## 7. THE LEGACY — Purpose Beyond Profit + Human Side

Alfred Lin is still active. He invests. He advises founders.

### What does he want to be remembered for?

In interviews, Lin emphasizes customer obsession and culture. He wants founders to focus on retention.

## 8. FINAL MOTIVATION & PERSONAL APPLICATION Guide

Alfred Lin's career demonstrates that customer obsession creates unicorns.

**5 Actionable Steps This Week:**

1. **Apply Lin's Law** — Before spending, ask: will this bring customers back?

2. **Focus on repeat customers** — Acquisition is expensive. Retention is profitable.

3. **Build culture** — Values matter when no one is looking. Define them.

4. **Obsess on experience** — Customer experience drives retention. Improve it.

5. **Learn from operators** — Lin was CFO/COO before VC. Operating experience matters.

**The Hard Question:**

If Lin builds unicorns through customer obsession—are you focused on acquisition or retention?

Customer obsession creates unicorns.
