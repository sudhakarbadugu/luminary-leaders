# Larry Page

**Co-founder, Google | CEO, Alphabet Inc. | Net Worth: $110+ Billion**

Larry Page is the visionary computer scientist who co-founded Google with Sergey Brin in 1998, transforming the company into one of the most powerful technology conglomerates in history. His leadership at Alphabet has been marked by bold bets on moonshot projects and a willingness to pursue ambitious, sometimes controversial, technological visions.

## Early Life and Academic Background

Lawrence Edward Page was born on March 26, 1973, in Lansing, Michigan. His father was a computer science professor at Michigan State University, and his mother was an electrical engineer and computer programming instructor. Page received his Bachelor of Science in Computer Engineering from the University of Michigan in 1995, then pursued graduate studies at Stanford University, where he earned a Master's degree in Computer Science. It was at Stanford that he met Sergey Brin, and together they began working on a research project that would eventually become Google.

## Founding Google and PageRank

Page's doctoral research focused on web crawler technology and the ranking of web pages. He developed the algorithm that would become Google PageRank, which evaluated the importance of websites based on the number and quality of links pointing to them. This revolutionary approach to search transformed the internet by delivering more relevant results than keyword matching alone. In 1998, Page and Brin founded Google as a company, with Page serving as CEO from 1998 to 2001, then again from 2011 to 2015. Under his leadership, Google expanded from a search engine into a multi-billion-dollar advertising, hardware, cloud, and AI powerhouse.

## Leading Alphabet and Moonshot Culture

After stepping down as Google CEO in 2015, Page became CEO of Alphabet, Google's parent company. He championed the "moonshot" philosophy that characterizes Alphabet's various subsidiaries: Waymo (self-driving cars), Verily (healthcare technology), Wing (drone delivery), and Calico (longevity research). Page believed that radical innovation required accepting large risks and long time horizons, a philosophy that attracted both brilliant engineers and significant regulatory scrutiny to the company.

## Personal Airship and Personal Interests

Page has maintained a reputation for extreme privacy and unusual personal projects. In 2017, it was revealed that he was funding the development of an enormous personal airship, described as one of the largest ever built, through a company called Kitty Hawk. The airship project reflected Page's interest in electric flight and personal transportation innovation. He has also invested heavily in electric aviation startups and alternative mobility solutions, reflecting a belief that transportation must become more sustainable and less dependent on fossil fuels.

## Management Style and Departure

Page's management style has been described as unconventional—he often delegated major decisions to division leaders rather than centralizing control. Some former employees characterized his approach as hands-off, though others noted his intense focus on specific projects that captured his interest. In 2019, Page stepped down as CEO of Alphabet, with Sundar Pichai taking over, though he remained on the board. His departure was marked by limited public communication, raising questions about his ongoing involvement in the company's direction.

## Philanthropy and Impact

Page has pledged substantial resources to philanthropy through the Google Foundation and personal charitable initiatives. He has funded climate change research, renewable energy projects, and urban development experiments. The footprint of Google's technology—from search to maps to translation—has fundamentally reshaped how humanity accesses and processes information, with Page's original vision of "organizing the world's information" largely achieved. His legacy includes both the transformation of digital advertising and the ongoing bet on transformative technology companies that may define the next century of human civilization.
