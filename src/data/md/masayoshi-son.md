# Masayoshi Son

## Early Life and Entrepreneurial Origins

Masayoshi Son was born on August 11, 1957, in Tosu, Saga Prefecture, Japan, the son of a fishing boat captain and a schoolteacher. He demonstrated exceptional intellectual abilities from childhood, graduating from UC Berkeley in just three years with a degree in economics in 1980, while simultaneously running several businesses. At age 19, Son developed a voice recognition computer system and sold the patent to Sharp for $1.7 million, establishing his reputation as a visionary entrepreneur capable of identifying emerging technology opportunities. He returned to Japan and founded SoftBank in 1981 with the ambition of creating a technology company that would become a major force in the global industry. The early years involved struggles and pivoting between various business ideas before finding his footing.

## Building SoftBank Empire

Son built SoftBank from a software distributor into one of Japan's most influential technology conglomerates through aggressive acquisitions and strategic investments. He acquired various rights to distribute software and gradually expanded into publishing, internet services, and telecommunications. The acquisition of a majority stake in Yahoo Japan in 1996 established SoftBank as a dominant force in the Japanese internet market. Son demonstrated an exceptional ability to identify promising technology trends and move aggressively to capitalize on them. His leadership style emphasized bold decision-making and willingness to make large bets on future technologies, characteristics that would define his later investment career. The company grew to encompass everything from mobile telecommunications to robotics and renewable energy.

## Vision Fund and Global Investment Strategy

In 2017, Son launched the SoftBank Vision Fund, raising over $100 billion from investors including Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund, Abu Dhabi, and major technology companies like Apple and Qualcomm. The fund represented a new paradigm in technology investing, deploying unprecedented amounts of capital into artificial intelligence, robotics, and other transformative technologies. Major investments included $4.4 billion to WeWork, $2.8 billion to滴滴出行 (Didi), and significant stakes in companies like Slack, DoorDash, and Uber. The Vision Fund strategy reflected Son's belief in funding "black swan" companies capable of reshaping entire industries. The scale of capital deployment gave Son enormous influence over the global technology startup ecosystem and venture capital industry dynamics.

## ARM Holdings Acquisition

In 2016, Son orchestrated SoftBank's acquisition of British semiconductor designer ARM Holdings for approximately $31 billion, representing one of the largest European technology acquisitions by a Japanese company. ARM designs the processor architectures used in virtually all smartphones globally, making it a strategically critical company in the technology supply chain. Son described the acquisition as positioning SoftBank for the coming artificial intelligence revolution, arguing that trillions of connected devices would require ARM's chip designs. The acquisition reflected Son's long-term technology vision and willingness to make major investments based on fundamental technological trends rather than short-term market conditions. Under SoftBank ownership, ARM continued expanding its reach into data centers, IoT devices, and automotive applications.

## Investment Philosophy and Risk-Taking

Son's investment approach is characterized by extreme patience combined with willingness to accept significant short-term losses for long-term gains. He famously spent ten years trying to convince Jack Ma to accept Alibaba investment before finally succeeding in 2000 with a $20 million stake that would eventually be worth over $100 billion. Son describes his strategy as "harma" (combining hard work andharma, a Buddhist concept), emphasizing persistence and long-term thinking. His approach accepts high failure rates in individual investments as inherent to searching for transformative successes. This philosophy has made him comfortable with large concentrated bets on companies with global ambitions, accepting significant volatility in pursuit of outsized returns.

## Personal Life and Family

Son married his business partner and longtime colleague but maintains relatively private family details despite his public profile as one of Japan's wealthiest individuals. He is a practicing Buddhist and credits his philosophy to both Buddhist teachings and American business thinking absorbed during his Berkeley years. Son has established various charitable initiatives through the SoftBank Foundation, particularly focusing on education and technology training in Japan. He has established scholarship programs supporting Japanese students studying abroad and entrepreneurs pursuing ambitious technology ventures. His personal wealth has fluctuated dramatically based on technology market valuations, but consistently ranks among Japan's wealthiest individuals.

## Challenges and Controversies

The Vision Fund faced significant challenges during 2019, with the high-profile failure of WeWork's IPO attempt forcing Son to personally bail out the company and replace its leadership. The near-collapse of WeWork exposed flaws in the fund's investment approach and due diligence processes, leading to restructuring and layoffs across SoftBank. Son acknowledged making mistakes in not challenging WeWork founder Adam Neumann more aggressively and accepted responsibility for the Vision Fund's difficulties. The COVID-19 pandemic created additional portfolio stress, but Son maintained his conviction in long-term technology investment despite market volatility. He has continued to raise subsequent Vision Funds while addressing lessons learned from earlier challenges.
