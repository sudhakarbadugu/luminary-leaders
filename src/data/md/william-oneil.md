## 2. ORIGIN — THE "Before" (The Relatability Engine)

William J. O'Neil was born March 25, 1933, in Oklahoma. He grew up during the Great Depression. The experience influenced his approach to risk.

He did not start with a fortune. He started with curiosity. He wanted to know: what do winning stocks have in common? He studied history. He studied data.

He was running from conventional wisdom. Most investors relied on tips, intuition, and stories. O'Neil relied on data.

## 3. THE FIRE — Failures, Rock-Bottom & Turning Points

### Computerizing Stock Data (1960s)

In the 1960s, O'Neil computerized stock market data. It was revolutionary. Wall Street was still using paper. O'Neil used computers to identify patterns.

### William O'Neil + Co.

He founded William O'Neil + Co., an institutional research firm. The firm provided data and research to major institutions.

### CAN SLIM

After studying thousands of winning stocks, O'Neil identified seven common traits. He codified them into CAN SLIM:
- **C**urrent Earnings: quarterly earnings up 25%+
- **A**nnual Earnings: consistent annual growth
- **N**ew Products: companies with innovation
- **S**upply/Demand: volume and float
- **L**eader: buy market leaders
- **I**nstitutional Sponsorship: smart money accumulation
- **M**arket Direction: the overall trend

### Investor's Business Daily (1984)

In 1984, O'Neil founded Investor's Business Daily. It democratized access to the data that institutions had. It gave individual investors the same information.

### How to Make Money in Stocks

The book became a bestseller. It taught millions of investors the CAN SLIM system.

## 4. THE GRIND — The Invisible Years (The Method)

O'Neil's method is defined by data, discipline, and cutting losses.

### Data-Driven

O'Neil studied history. He asked: what do winning stocks have in common? He did not rely on opinion. He relied on data.

### The 7-8% Rule

O'Neil's famous rule: cut losses at 7-8% below purchase price. No exceptions. If you are wrong, exit. Preserve capital.

### Pattern Recognition

O'Neil documented chart patterns. The most famous: the cup-with-handle. A consolidation pattern that precedes breakouts.

### Market Direction

O'Neil emphasized that the overall market determines 70-80% of a stock's movement. You cannot fight the trend.

The invisible years—the years of studying data—were years of pattern recognition. O'Neil did not guess. He observed.
