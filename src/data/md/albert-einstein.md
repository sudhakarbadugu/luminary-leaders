# Albert Einstein — Theory of Relativity

## 1. THE HOOK

He developed the Theory of Relativity and changed our understanding of space, time, and energy. He won the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1921. He fled Nazi Germany and became an American citizen. He says: "Imagination is more important than knowledge."

How does a patent clerk in Bern, Switzerland, revolutionize our understanding of the universe?

## 2. ORIGIN — THE "Before" (The Relatability Engine)

Albert Einstein was born March 14, 1879, in Ulm, in the Kingdom of Württemberg in the German Empire. His father, Hermann Einstein, was a featherbed salesman. His mother, Pauline Koch, was a pianist. The family moved to Munich when Albert was one year old.

Einstein was a late talker. His parents worried. At five, his father showed him a magnetic compass. He was fascinated—something invisible was making the needle move. He later called this his "wonder year."

In school, he was rebellious. He hated rote learning. He read popular science books. He studied mathematics and philosophy. His teachers called him lazy and disruptive. He failed his first entrance exam to the Swiss Federal Polytechnic in Zurich (1895) in French, botany, and chemistry. He passed the second time.

At Zurich, he met Mileva Marić, a Serbian physics student. They married in 1903. They had two sons: Hans Albert (1904) and Eduard (1910). The marriage was troubled. They divorced in 1919. Einstein later married his cousin Elsa Löwenthal (1919).

## 3. THE FIRE — Failures, Rock-Bottom & Turning Points

### The Annus Mirabilis (1905)

Einstein worked as a patent clerk in Bern. In his spare time, he wrote four papers—published between March and June 1905. These were:

1. Photoelectric effect: Light comes in packets (quanta). This earned the Nobel Prize.
2. Brownian motion: Atoms exist and can be measured.
3. Special relativity: Time and space are not fixed—they depend on the observer's speed.
4. Mass-energy equivalence: E=mc².

He was 26. He changed physics in one year.

### The Academic Struggle (1908-1911)

Einstein tried to become a professor. He was rejected by the University of Bern. He applied for teaching positions and was turned down repeatedly. He became a professor at the University of Zurich in 1909. Then German University in Prague. Then ETH Zurich. His work was recognized slowly.

He was nominated for the Nobel Prize nine times before winning in 1921 for the photoelectric effect. Special relativity was still too controversial for the committee.

### The Berlin Years (1914-1933)

Einstein moved to Berlin in 1914 to direct the Kaiser Wilhelm Institute for Physics. He worked on general relativity—his masterpiece. The theory predicted that gravity bends light. British astronomer Arthur Eddington tested this during a 1919 solar eclipse. The sun's light bent exactly as Einstein predicted. The London Times headline: "Revolution in Science—Einstein Theory Triumphant."

Einstein became world-famous overnight. He toured America. He met presidents. He raised money for the Hebrew University of Jerusalem.

But he also made enemies. Right-wing Germans called his work "Jewish physics." In 1933, the Nazis burned his books. His property was confiscated. He was 54. He would never return to Germany.

### The Atomic Bomb (1939-1945)

Einstein signed a letter to President Roosevelt in 1939, warning that Germany might build an atomic bomb. He did not work on the Manhattan Project. He never visited Los Alamos. He was denied security clearance.

He learned about Hiroshima from a newspaper headline. He wrote: "I could not help cursing the development."

He spent his later years campaigning for nuclear disarmament.

## 4. THE GRIND — The Invisible Years (The Method)

Einstein's method was independent thinking and visual imagination.

### Thought Experiments

Einstein imagined riding on a beam of light. He pictured a closed elevator accelerating through space—and that it would feel identical to gravity. He imagined twins aging at different speeds. He used these pictures to develop relativity.

He said: "The脑子里有了画面，文字和数学自然跟上。" (The visual imagination came first—the words and math followed.)

### The Zurich Notebook

Einstein's private notebooks from 1905-1915 show his mistakes, his struggles, his incremental progress. He made dozens of errors. He crossed out pages. He rewrote equations. He worked alone for years.

He said: "I have no special talent. I am only passionately curious."

### The Public Figure

Einstein became a celebrity. He played violin for charity. He gave interviews. He wrote articles for popular magazines. He supported civil rights, socialism, and pacifism. He was asked to be president of Israel in 1952. He declined.

He said: "If I would be a young man again and had to decide how to make my living, I would not try to become a scientist or scholar or teacher. I would rather choose to be a plumber."

## 5. THE TEST — THE BREAKTHROUGH MOMENT

### April 18, 1955

Albert Einstein died at Princeton Hospital in Princeton, New Jersey, at age 76. He had been diagnosed with an abdominal aortic aneurysm in 1949. He refused surgery: "I want to go when I want. It is tasteless to prolong life artificially. I have done my share, it is time to go."

He was buried in Princeton Cemetery. His ashes were scattered. His brain was preserved for study by Thomas Harvey, the pathologist. It was stolen, studied, and eventually returned to his family.

## 6. THE PHILOSOPHY — Stealable Wisdom

**On Imagination:**
"Imagination is more important than knowledge. Knowledge is limited. Imagination encircles the world."

**On Curiosity:**
"I have no special talent. I am only passionately curious."

**On Failure:**
"Anyone who has never made a mistake has never tried anything new."

**On Peace:**
"Peace cannot be kept by force. It can only be achieved by understanding."

## 7. THE LEGACY — Purpose Beyond Profit + Human Side

Einstein's work transformed physics. His E=mc² equation enabled nuclear energy and nuclear weapons. His photoelectric effect enabled semiconductors and solar cells. His relativity theory enables GPS navigation.

He left all his papers to the Hebrew University of Jerusalem. The Einstein Archives contain 80,000 documents.

He was a refugee twice—from Germany and from McCarthy-era America. He supported the NAACP, the American Civil Liberties Union, and the Hollywood Ten. He was surveilled by the FBI for 22 years—2,000 pages of documents.

He wrote to President Eisenhower in 1955 proposing a world government. He was called naive. He was right about nuclear proliferation.

He remains the most recognizable scientist in history. His image appears on T-shirts, posters, coffee mugs. His wild hair and rumpled sweaters are iconic. He was a human being who made mistakes, struggled with relationships, and changed our understanding of the universe.

## 8. FINAL MOTIVATION & Personal Application Guide

Einstein's life proves that imagination beats knowledge.

**5 Actionable Steps This Week:**

1. **Think visually** — Einstein imagined riding a light beam. Picture your problems. Don't just calculate.

2. **Question everything** — "Why?" is the start of all breakthroughs. Challenge assumptions.

3. **Work in private** — Einstein wrote the Annus Mirabilis papers in his spare time. Master your craft before seeking recognition.

4. **Persist after rejection** — He was rejected for academic positions for years. Keep going.

5. **Use your fame for good** — Einstein advocated for peace and civil rights. Your platform matters.

**The Hard Question:**

Einstein said imagination is more important than knowledge. What are you imagining—and what are you just memorizing?
