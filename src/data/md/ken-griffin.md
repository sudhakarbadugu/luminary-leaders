# Ken Griffin — The Quantitative Titan Who Built a Financial Empire

## 1. THE HOOK

In 1987, he was trading convertible bond arbitrage from his Harvard dorm room. In 1990, he founded Citadel with $4.1 million. Today, Citadel manages approximately $60 billion. Citadel Securities handles approximately 25% of US stock trading volume. He returned 20%+ annualized for decades. He survived the 2008 financial crisis by locking the gates—restricting redemptions when others were collapsing. His net worth: $35-40 billion. His philosophy: "We're a technology company that happens to be in finance." He is Ken Griffin. He built one of the largest hedge funds and market makers in the world.

How does a college student trading from his dorm room build a $60 billion empire?

## 2. ORIGIN — THE "Before" (The Relatability Engine)

Ken Griffin was born October 15, 1968, in Daytona Beach, Florida. He attended Harvard University. In 1987, as a freshman, he started trading convertible bond arbitrage from his dorm room.

He was running from conventional careers. He could have gone to investment banking. He chose to start his own firm.

## 3. THE FIRE — Failures, Rock-Bottom & Turning Points

### Trading from the Dorm Room (1987)

In 1987, Griffin started trading from his Harvard dorm room. He used convertible bond arbitrage. He was successful.

### Founding Citadel (1990)

In 1990, Griffin founded Citadel with $4.1 million. He was 22 years old. The name "Citadel" reflected his philosophy: build a fortress.

### Performance

Citadel returned approximately 20%+ annualized for decades. It is one of the largest hedge funds globally.

### Citadel Securities

Citadel Securities became one of the largest market makers in the US. It handles approximately 25% of US stock trading volume.

### The 2008 Crisis

In 2008, Griffin restricted investor redemptions. He locked the gates. He prevented forced liquidation. Citadel survived. It thrived post-crisis.
