# Jim Simons — The Mathematician Who Beat the Market With Equations

## 1. THE HOOK

He was a mathematician. He worked on string theory. He broke codes for the US government during the Vietnam War. He was a professor and a department chair. At age 40, he decided to start a hedge fund. Most hedge funds hire MBAs and traders. Simons hired physicists and mathematicians. He told them to find patterns in market data. They did. His fund, the Medallion Fund, has returned approximately 39% per year after fees for over 30 years. No fund in history has been more successful. Jim Simons did not beat the market by being smarter about companies. He beat it by being smarter about math.

How does a mathematician build the best-performing hedge fund in history?

## 2. ORIGIN — The "Before" (The Relatability Engine)

James Harris Simons was born April 25, 1938, in Newton, Massachusetts. His family was middle class. He was interested in mathematics from a young age. He studied mathematics at MIT and later at the University of California, Berkeley, where he earned a PhD.

He taught mathematics at MIT and Harvard. In 1968, at age 30, he became the chair of the mathematics department at Stony Brook University. He worked on geometry and topology. He collaborated with Shiing-Shen Chern on what became known as the Chern-Simons form—a contribution to string theory that is still studied today.

During the Vietnam War, Simons worked for the Institute for Defense Analyses, a US government think tank, breaking codes. He was fired in 1968 after opposing the war in Vietnam.

He was running from a conventional academic career. He was a successful mathematician. But he wanted to try something different. At 40, he left academia to start a hedge fund.

## 3. THE FIRE — Failures, Rock-Bottom & Turning Points

### Renaissance Technologies (1982)

In 1982, Simons founded Renaissance Technologies. Initially, the fund traded like other funds—using intuition, fundamentals, and some quantitative methods. It did well but not extraordinarily.

In 1988, Simons launched the Medallion Fund. It was different. Simons hired physicists, mathematicians, and computer scientists. He did not hire MBAs or traditional traders. He told them to find patterns in market data. The models were built in secret. They were proprietary. No one outside Renaissance knew exactly how they worked.

### Medallion Fund Returns

The returns were unprecedented. Over three decades, the Medallion Fund returned approximately 66% annually before fees and 39% after fees. No other fund has come close. The fund is closed to outside investors—it is only available to Renaissance employees.

### Philanthropy

Simons and his wife, Marilyn, founded the Simons Foundation, which has donated more than $3 billion to mathematics, science, and autism research. Simons has been a major funder of basic research in mathematics and physics.

## 4. THE GRIND — The Invisible Years (The Method)

Simons's method is defined by mathematics, data, and secrecy.

### Quantitative Trading

Simons did not trade on intuition. He traded on patterns discovered in data. Renaissance's models analyze market data, identify statistical anomalies, and execute trades. The models are updated constantly. The approach is sometimes called "black box" trading because the methods are not disclosed.

### Hiring Non-Traditional Talent

Simons hired physicists, mathematicians, and computer scientists. He did not hire finance professionals. He believed that physicists and mathematicians were trained to find patterns in noisy data. Markets are noisy. The approach worked.

### Secrecy

Renaissance is famously secretive. The Medallion Fund's methods are not public. Simons gave few interviews. The firm does not disclose details of its models. The secrecy is not just competitive advantage—it is the product.

The invisible years—the years before Medallion—were years of building the team and the models. Simons spent a decade hiring mathematicians and letting them experiment with market data.

## 5. THE TEST — The Breakthrough Moment

The Medallion Fund's performance is the test. Over 30 years, it has returned approximately 39% annually after fees. No other fund has matched this. If the efficient market hypothesis were strictly true, such performance would be impossible. The Medallion Fund is evidence that markets are not perfectly efficient—that there are patterns to be found, if you have the right algorithms and the right people.

## 6. THE PHILOSOPHY — Stealable Wisdom

**On Hiring:**
Simons hired physicists and mathematicians, not MBAs. He believed that the skills required to find patterns in data are different from the skills required to value companies.

**On Secrecy:**
Renaissance does not disclose its methods. Competitive advantage in trading comes from having models that others do not have. If everyone knows the model, the inefficiency disappears.

**On Mathematics:**
Simons has said that he looks for patterns in data, not stories about companies. If there is a statistical relationship, you can trade it. You do not need to understand why it exists.

**Key Quotes:**
- "I don't want to be a billionaire. I just want to be able to do what I want."
- "The only way to do anything interesting is to do it when you don't know what the outcome will be."

## 7. THE LEGACY — Purpose Beyond Profit + Human Side

Jim Simons is retired from day-to-day management. He remains involved as non-executive chairman.

### Philanthropy

Through the Simons Foundation, he has donated billions to mathematics, science, and autism research. He has funded autism research because his daughter has autism. He has funded mathematics because he loves the subject.

### Wealth

Simons's net worth is estimated at $28-30 billion. He is one of the wealthiest mathematicians in history.

### What does he regret?

Simons has been asked about whether his wealth was worth it. He has said that he did not set out to become a billionaire. He set out to make money and be independent. The billions were a byproduct.

### What does he want to be remembered for?

In interviews, Simons has said he wants to be remembered for his contributions to mathematics and science—both through his own research and through the Simons Foundation.

## 8. FINAL MOTIVATION & PERSONAL APPLICATION Guide

Jim Simons's career demonstrates that the skills required to succeed in quantitative trading are not the skills taught in business school. Physics and mathematics train you to find patterns. Markets have patterns.

**5 Actionable Steps This Week:**

1. **Hire for skills, not credentials** — Simons hired physicists and mathematicians, not MBAs. If you are building a quantitative team, hire people who can find patterns in noisy data.

2. **Find patterns, don't tell stories** — Simons did not trade on stories about companies. He traded on statistical relationships. If you want to be systematic, find patterns in data.

3. **Keep your methods secret** — Renaissance does not disclose its models. Competitive advantage comes from having methods others do not have.

4. **Give back** — Simons has given away billions to mathematics and science. If you are successful, fund what you love.

5. **Do it when you don't know the outcome** — Simons left academia at 40 to start a hedge fund. He did not know it would work. If you want to do something interesting, accept uncertainty.

**The Hard Question:**

If a mathematician could beat the market by finding patterns in noisy data—what patterns are you not seeing because you are looking at stories instead of numbers?

The numbers have patterns. Find them.
