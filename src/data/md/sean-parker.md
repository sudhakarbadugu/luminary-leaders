# Sean Parker — The Disruptor Who Cofounded Napster and Shaped Facebook

## 1. THE HOOK

At 19, he cofounded Napster. The music industry sued him into bankruptcy. At 24, he became Facebook's founding president. He was forced to resign after a cocaine arrest. He invested early in Spotify. He became a billionaire. He says: "Running a start-up is like eating glass. You just start to like the taste of your own blood."

How does a dropout build and lose companies and come back?

## 2. ORIGIN — THE "Before" (The Relatability Engine)

Sean Parker was born December 3, 1979, in Herndon, Virginia. He studied at Virginia public schools (dropped out before high school graduation).

He was running from formal education. He saw internet disruption. He wanted to challenge industries.

## 3. THE FIRE — Failures, Rock-Bottom & Turning Points

### Napster (1999)

In 1999, Parker cofounded Napster with Shawn Fanning. Peer-to-peer music sharing. 80 million users. Sued by record labels. Bankrupt.

### Plaxo (2002)

In 2002, Parker cofounded Plaxo. Contact management. Fired from his own company by investors.

### Facebook (2004-2005)

In 2004, Parker became Facebook's founding president. He helped Mark Zuckerberg. In 2005, cocaine arrest. Forced to resign.

### Spotify Investor

Parker invested early in Spotify. Helped negotiate with record labels.

### Founders Fund

Parker became founding partner of Founders Fund. Invested in Airbnb, SpaceX, Lyft.

## 4. THE GRIND — The Invisible Years (The Method)

Parker's method is defined by disruption, spotting opportunities, and resilience.

### Disruption

Parker's philosophy: Challenge entrenched industries. Napster disrupted music.

### Spotting Opportunities

Parker's philosophy: "A few times in your life, you have these moments of clarity where all the stars align."

### Resilience

Parker's philosophy: "Running a start-up is like eating glass. You just start to like the taste of your own blood."

The invisible years—the years after Napster—gave Parker Plaxo. After Plaxo firing, Facebook.

## 5. THE TEST — THE BREAKTHROUGH MOMENT

Facebook founding president is the test. Napster disrupted music. Spotify investment. Resilience works.

Sean Parker's career demonstrates that disruption creates value despite failures.
