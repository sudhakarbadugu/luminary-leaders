# Ed Catmull — The Founder Who Made Pixar Creative

## 1. THE HOOK

In 1986, he cofounded Pixar with Steve Jobs and John Lasseter. He developed foundational computer graphics techniques: texture mapping, anti-aliasing. He created the Braintrust feedback system. He wrote "Creativity Inc." He sold Pixar to Disney in 2006 for $7.4 billion. He says: "Quality is the best business model."

How does a computer scientist build the world's greatest animation studio?

## 2. ORIGIN — THE "Before" (The Relatability Engine)

Ed Catmull was born 1941 in America. He studied at University of Utah (BS, PhD computer science). He developed foundational CG techniques at Utah.

He was running from traditional animation. He saw computer graphics. He wanted to make the first CGI film.

## 3. THE FIRE — Failures, Rock-Bottom & Turning Points

### University of Utah

Catmull developed texture mapping, anti-aliasing. Foundational CG techniques.

### Lucasfilm (1979-1986)

In 1979, Catmull joined Lucasfilm Computer Graphics Group. Lucas sold it to Steve Jobs in 1986.

### Pixar Cofounder (1986)

In 1986, Catmull cofounded Pixar with Jobs and Lasseter. He became president.

### Pixar Films

Pixar made Toy Story (1995). First CGI feature. Followed by A Bug's Life, Toy Story 2, Monsters Inc, Finding Nemo, The Incredibles, Cars, Ratatouille, WALL-E, Up.

### Disney Acquisition (2006)

In 2006, Disney acquired Pixar for $7.4 billion. Catmull became president of Walt Disney Animation Studios.

### Creativity Inc. (2014)

In 2014, Catmull wrote "Creativity Inc." with Amy Wallace. Braintrust, fail fast, postmortems.

## 4. THE GRIND — The Invisible Years (The Method)

Catmull's method is defined by Braintrust, fail fast, and protect creatives.

### Braintrust

Catmull's philosophy: Feedback from trusted peers. Honest. Candid. No hierarchy.

### Fail Fast

Catmull's philosophy: Fail fast. Learn quickly. Iterate.

### Protect Creatives

Catmull's philosophy: Shield creative teams from executives. Creativity needs safety.

The invisible years—the years of CG research—gave Catmull technical credibility. He built Pixar.

## 5. THE TEST — THE BREAKthrough Moment

$7.4 billion acquisition is the test. Toy Story first CGI feature. Quality is the best business model.

## 6. THE PHILOSOPHY — Stealable Wisdom

**On Quality:**
"Quality is the best business model."

**On Storytelling:**
"Storytelling is king. We say that at Pixar and Disney, and we mean it."

**On Teams:**
"Give a good idea to a mediocre team, and they will screw it up. Give a mediocre idea to a great team, and they will either fix it or come up with something better."

## 7. THE LEGACY — Purpose Beyond Profit + Human Side

Ed Catmull retired in 2019. He wrote Creativity Inc. He advises.

### What does he want to be remembered for?

In interviews, Catmull emphasizes creativity and process. He wants to protect creative teams.

## 8. FINAL MOTIVATION & PERSONAL APPLICATION Guide

Ed Catmull's career demonstrates that process enables creativity.

**5 Actionable Steps This Week:**

1. **Create a Braintrust** — Feedback from trusted peers. Honest. No hierarchy.

2. **Fail fast** — Catmull embraced failure. Learn quickly. Iterate.

3. **Protect creatives** — Shield teams from executives. Safety enables creativity.

4. **Prioritize quality** — "Quality is the best business model." Don't compromise.

5. **Run postmortems** — Catmull ran postmortems after every film. Learn from mistakes.

**The Hard Question:**

If Catmull built Pixar on creativity—are you protecting your creative teams?

Process enables creativity.
