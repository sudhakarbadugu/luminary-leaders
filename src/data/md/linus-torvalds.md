# Linus Torvalds

**Creator of Linux and Git | Net Worth: $500+ Million**

Linus Torvalds is the Finnish-American software engineer who created Linux, the open-source operating system that powers most of the world's servers, smartphones, and supercomputers. His leadership of the global Linux development community and his creation of Git—the version control system used by virtually every software developer—have made him one of the most influential figures in computing history.

## Early Life and Education

Linus Benedict Torvalds was born on December 28, 1969, in Helsinki, Finland. His grandfather, a university professor, introduced him to programming on an early computer when he was just eleven years old. Torvalds studied computer science at the University of Helsinki, where he began developing his own operating system kernel as a hobby project in 1991. He released the first version of Linux (originally called "Freax") to the internet in August 1991, inviting other programmers to contribute to the project—a collaboration model that would define open-source software development for decades.

## Creating Linux

Linux started as a personal project to understand how operating systems worked, but it quickly grew into something far larger. Torvalds' kernel, combined with GNU tools and other software components, created a complete Unix-like operating system distributed freely under the GPL license. The openness of Linux attracted thousands of contributors, and the operating system evolved from a academic project into a production-grade system used by companies like Google, Amazon, IBM, and Microsoft. Today, Linux runs approximately 75% of servers worldwide, 90%+ of the top 500 supercomputers, and the Android operating system that powers most smartphones.

## Git and Version Control Revolution

In 2005, Torvalds created Git after the proprietary BitKeeper version control system revoked its free license for the Linux kernel development community. Torvalds designed Git for speed, data integrity, and support for distributed, non-linear workflows—requirements that the existing version control systems could not meet. Git became the standard for software version control, enabling the modern open-source collaboration model. Platforms like GitHub and GitLab built on Git's foundation, creating ecosystems where millions of developers collaborate daily.

## Leadership Philosophy

Torvalds is known for his direct and sometimes abrasive communication style, famously issuing harsh criticism in developer mailing lists and maintaining exacting standards for code quality. Despite this intensity, his leadership has been remarkably effective at building and maintaining global developer communities. He famously coined "Linus's Law": "Given enough eyeballs, all bugs are shallow"—the idea that distributed review by many developers catches errors more effectively than any formal testing process. He stepped back from daily kernel maintenance in 2022, delegating to trusted maintainers while remaining the final authority on controversial decisions.

## Recognition and Impact

Torvalds received the EFF Pioneer Award in 2012 and the fellow grade of the IEEE in 2019. He declined the Finnish Order of the White Rose, reportedly finding the bureaucratic process too burdensome. In 2014, he was inducted into the Internet Hall of Fame. His creation of Linux and Git fundamentally changed how software is developed and distributed, enabling companies like Google and Amazon to build global internet infrastructure on freely available components. His influence extends beyond code—his model of open, meritocratic collaboration has become the dominant paradigm in modern software development.
