# Ingvar Kamprad
## Founder of IKEA, Furniture Empire Builder

### Introduction
Ingvar Feuze Kamprad was a Swedish business magnate who founded IKEA, the world's largest furniture retailer. Born in 1926 in Pjätteryd, Sweden, he started his business career at age 17 with a mail-order seed operation. From these humble beginnings, he built a global empire that revolutionized affordable home furnishing and changed how millions of people furnish their homes. His leadership principles of frugality, simplicity, and customer focus became embedded in IKEA's DNA and continue to guide the company today.

### Early Life & Background
Kamprad grew up on a farm in southern Sweden. His father was a dairy farmer who instilled in him a strong work ethic and financial discipline from an early age. At age 6, Ingvar began selling matches to neighbors, soon expanding to seeds, and later Christmas decorations. By 17, he had founded IKEA (initially stnood for Ingvar Kamprad, Elmtaryd, Agunnaryd—his farm and hometown). His entrepreneurial instincts were apparent even in his youth, as he constantly sought opportunities to buy low and sell high in his local community.

### Career & Achievements
IKEA was founded in 1943, initially as a mail-order business selling watches, clocks, and later furniture. The turning point came in 1952 when Kamprad introduced flat-pack furniture, a revolutionary concept that allowed customers to transport and assemble furniture themselves, dramatically reducing costs. By the 1960s, IKEA had expanded across Europe, and today it operates in over 50 countries with more than 450 stores. Under Kamprad's leadership, IKEA became known for its democratic design—beautiful, functional furniture at prices affordable for the many, not just the few.

### Leadership Philosophy
Kamprad's leadership was defined by relentless frugality. He famously flew economy class, drove old Volvo, and banned unnecessary expenses across his organization. He believed that waste was a sin and that resources should always be used efficiently. His philosophy extended beyond cost-cutting to product design—every IKEA product must deliver maximum value at minimum cost. He institutionalized the principle that great design is not a luxury but a right that should be accessible to everyone. His famous quote, "Waste of resources is a mortal sin," became a foundational belief across the company.

### Key Decisions & Milestones
In 1956, a pivotal moment occurred when a colleague suggested removing table legs to make transportation easier—this sparked the flat-pack revolution. Kamprad decentralized decision-making, empowering store managers to act autonomously. He made the controversial but brilliant move of establishing IKEA's own stores rather than relying on intermediaries, controlling the entire customer experience. In 1986, he stepped down as CEO but remained active as senior advisor. He structured IKEA with a complex ownership model to protect it from being acquired or broken apart.

### Legacy & Impact
Ingvar Kamprad transformed global retail and made stylish, affordable furniture accessible to billions. His flat-pack model influenced the entire furniture industry and inspired countless other businesses. IKEA's environmental initiatives, including sustainable forestry and recycling programs, reflect his belief in responsible resource use. He turned a small Swedish company into a cultural phenomenon, with IKEA cafes, showrooms, and the iconic Swedish meatballs becoming household names worldwide. His leadership case study is taught in business schools globally.

### Personal Life
Kamprad was married twice and had four children. Despite his billions, he maintained an almost ascetic lifestyle, living in a modest apartment in Switzerland and flying economy. He was known for his simplicity—he once said, "I don't need a nice car, a beautiful home, or luxury goods." He was passionate about golf and believed in early mornings and hard work. He also struggled with dyslexia, which he later considered an asset that forced him to think differently and delegate effectively.
