# Andrej Karpathy — AI Researcher and Educator

## 1. THE HOOK

He built Tesla's Autopilot and OpenAI's early systems. He taught neural networks to millions. He says: "The most important skill is learning how to learn."

How does a Canadian PhD student become one of the most influential AI researchers in the world—and then make AI education accessible to millions?

## 2. ORIGIN — THE "Before" (The Relatability Engine)

Andrej Karpathy was born October 23, 1985, in Bratislava, Czechoslovakia (now Slovakia). His father was an engineer. His mother was a pharmacist. When he was ten, the family emigrated to Canada—part of the wave after the Velvet Revolution. They settled in Toronto, then Vancouver.

Karpathy was fascinated by math and physics from an early age. He won national competitions in programming. At 15, he built a video game from scratch. At 17, he won the International Physics Olympiad.

He studied computer science and physics at the University of British Columbia. He graduated with first-class honors in 2009. He went to Stanford for his PhD, studying deep learning under Fei-Fei Li.

At Stanford, he worked on convolutional neural networks for image recognition. He built a system that could describe images in natural language—neural image captioning. He demonstrated that deep learning could understand images and generate text about them.

He completed his PhD in 2015. He had published 17 papers. Stanford named him theOutstanding doctoral dissertation of the year. He was 29.

## 3. THE FIRE — Failures, Rock-Bottom & Turning Points

### The ImageNet Moment (2012-2014)

During Karpathy's PhD, deep learning was still controversial. The AI community doubted neural networks could scale. Then in 2012, Alex Krizhevsky, Ilya Sutskever, and Geoffrey Hinton won the ImageNet competition with a convolutional neural network—the field changed overnight.

Karpathy had bet on the right horse. But he still struggled to publish his work. Reviewers said neural networks were "not interpretable" and "not rigorous." He argued that performance mattered more than interpretability. He was right.

He co-founded the Stanford course CS231n (Convolutional Neural Networks for Visual Recognition) with Justin Johnson and Serena Yeung in 2015. It became the most popular AI course at Stanford—700 students in the first quarter.

### The Tesla Autopilot (2017-2022)

Karpathy joined Tesla as Director of AI in July 2017. He was recruited by Elon Musk to lead the Autopilot team. He built the computer vision system for Tesla's self-driving cars.

At Tesla, he faced immense pressure. Musk pushed for full self-driving by 2018. The timeline was unrealistic. Karpathy had to manage expectations, deliver results, and keep the team together.

In 2018, a Tesla Model X crashed on Autopilot, killing the driver. The media blamed the technology. Karpathy defended it publicly: "We are making progress. The system is improving. But autonomy is hard."

He built the data pipeline, the training infrastructure, and the neural network architecture. He hired hundreds of engineers. He presented at Tesla's Autonomy Day events. He was the public face of Tesla's AI.

In July 2022, Karpathy announced he was leaving Tesla. He said he was taking time off to travel and recharge. The announcement was amicable. Musk tweeted: "Thanks for everything. Great working with you."

### The AI Education (2015-2023)

After leaving Tesla, Karpathy returned to education. He built "zero-to-hero" AI tutorials on YouTube. His videos on neural networks, backpropagation, and transformers have over 50 million views.

He built a 6-hour video course called "Neural Networks from Scratch in Python." It teaches the fundamentals of deep learning without libraries. He believed: "You can't understand AI until you build it yourself."

His blog became a resource for millions of students worldwide.

## 4. THE GRIND — The Invisible Years (The Method)

Karpathy's method was building from scratch and explaining clearly.

### Build From Scratch

Karpathy believes in building everything from scratch to understand it. He wrote his own matrix multiplication library in graduate school. He built his own backpropagation algorithm. He re-implemented every paper he read.

He says: "If you can't implement it from memory, you don't understand it."

This approach made him one of the best debugging engineers in AI. He could trace errors through complex systems. He could identify where gradients were exploding or vanishing.

### The Tesla Team

Karpathy built Tesla's AI team from scratch. He hired 300+ engineers across San Francisco, Texas, and other locations. He established a culture of "craftsmanship and speed." He insisted on code reviews and testing.

He says: "At Tesla, we move fast. But we don't move fast at the expense of correctness."

### The Education System

Karpathy's education content is famous for its clarity. He uses analogies, visualizations, and animations. He breaks down complex math into simple steps.

He says: "The goal is to make AI accessible. Not just to PhDs, but to anyone who wants to learn."

## 5. THE TEST — THE BREAKTHROUGH MOMENT

### The OpenAI Years (2015-2017)

Karpathy joined OpenAI as Research Scientist in 2015. He was one of the first employees—before it became famous. OpenAI was backed by Elon Musk, Sam Altman, and others. The goal: build safe AI for humanity.

At OpenAI, Karpathy worked on reinforcement learning and robotics. He built systems that could learn to manipulate objects. He co-authored papers on generative models.

He stayed for two years, then left for Tesla. OpenAI later became one of the most valuable AI companies in the world, creating ChatGPT and DALL-E.

## 7. THE LEGACY — Purpose Beyond Profit + Human Side

Karpathy has published over 50 papers on deep learning, computer vision, and reinforcement learning. His research has been cited over 50,000 times. He has taught over 50 million people through his courses and tutorials.

He is one of the most followed people on Twitter/X in AI—over 1 million followers. He gives talks at conferences, podcasts, and universities. He mentors young researchers.

He remains humble. He still answers questions on Reddit and Twitter. He still builds tutorials in his spare time.

He says: "I want to understand AI deeply. I want to help others understand it too."

## 8. FINAL MOTIVATION & Personal Application Guide

Karpathy learned AI by building from scratch. His life proves that deep understanding comes from implementation, not theory.

4. **Move between theory and practice** — Karpathy at Stanford, Tesla, OpenAI. Apply theory to real systems.

5. **Build for everyone** — Karpathy's tutorials are free. Make knowledge accessible.
