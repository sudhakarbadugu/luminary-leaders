# Ash Maurya — The Creator of Lean Canvas

## 1. THE HOOK

He created Lean Canvas—a 1-page business plan adapted from Business Model Canvas. He wrote "Running Lean" in 2012. He wrote "Scaling Lean" in 2016. He was influenced by Eric Ries, Alexander Osterwalder, Steve Blank. He says: "Your job is not to build a product. Your job is to solve a problem." He focuses on validation over building. He is Ash Maurya.

How does an entrepreneur create the simplest startup tool?

## 2. ORIGIN — THE "Before" (The Relatability Engine)

Ash Maurya is an American entrepreneur. He experienced startup struggles. He learned from failure.

He was running from unnecessary complexity. He saw that founders needed simpler tools. He adapted the Business Model Canvas.

## 3. THE FIRE — Failures, Rock-Bottom & Turning Points

### Lean Canvas

Maurya created Lean Canvas. He adapted Alexander Osterwalder's Business Model Canvas for startups. He removed enterprise-focused elements. He focused on problem-solution fit.

### Running Lean (2012)

In 2012, Maurya wrote "Running Lean." It is a step-by-step guide for Lean Startup methodology.

### Scaling Lean (2016)

In 2016, Maurya wrote "Scaling Lean." It addresses growth and scaling challenges.

## 4. THE GRIND — The Invisible Years (The Method)

Maurya's method is defined by problem-first, rapid experimentation, and validation.

### Problem-First Mindset

Maurya's philosophy: "Your job is not to build a product. Your job is to solve a problem." Understand pain points first.

### Rapid Experimentation

Maurya advocates testing assumptions before investing heavily. Iterate fast.

### Measure Over Build

Maurya's philosophy: "Stop building and start measuring." Validated learning over feature development.

The invisible years—the years of startup struggles—taught Maurya what founders need. Simplicity. Validation.

## 5. THE TEST — The Breakthrough Moment

Lean Canvas is the test. It became one of the most widely-used strategic planning tools in the startup ecosystem.
