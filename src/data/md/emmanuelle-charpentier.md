# Emmanuelle Charpentier — The Nobel Laureate Who Discovered CRISPR Scissors

## 1. THE HOOK

In 2020, she won the Nobel Prize in Chemistry for CRISPR-Cas9 gene editing. With Jennifer Doudna, she discovered "molecular scissors" that can edit DNA. She is a French microbiologist working in Germany.

How does a microbiologist change the future of genetic medicine?

## 2. ORIGIN — THE "Before" (The Relatability Engine)

Emmanuelle Charpentier was born December 11, 1968, in Juvisy-sur-Orge, France. She studied at University Pierre and Marie Curie (PhD microbiology).

She was running from traditional biology. She saw bacterial immune systems. She wanted to understand CRISPR.

## 3. THE FIRE — Failures, Rock-Bottom & Turning Points

### University of Vienna, Umeå University

Charpentier worked at University of Vienna and Umeå University Sweden. Bacterial defense mechanisms.

### CRISPR Research

Charpentier studied CRISPR-Cas immune systems. How bacteria defend against viruses.

### CRISPR-Cas9 Discovery (2012)

In 2012, Charpentier and Doudna published CRISPR-Cas9 in Science. Gene editing breakthrough. "Molecular scissors." Edit DNA.

### Max Planck Institute

Charpentier became director at Max Planck Institute for Infection Biology in Berlin.

### CRISPR Therapeutics

Charpentier cofounded CRISPR Therapeutics. Gene editing therapies.

### Nobel Prize (2020)

In 2020, Charpentier won the Nobel Prize in Chemistry with Jennifer Doudna. First all-female science Nobel team.
