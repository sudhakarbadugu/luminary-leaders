# Marty Schwartz — The Pit Bull Who Won the US Investing Championship

## 1. THE HOOK

In 1984, he entered the US Investing Championship. He returned 175%. He won. He turned $40,000 into over $2 million over 10 years trading S&P futures. He never worked on Wall Street. He traded from his beach house in California. He was featured in Jack Schwager's "Market Wizards." His nickname: The Pit Bull. Marty Schwartz was a day trader. He did not hold positions overnight. He used technical analysis. He said: "Before I place a trade, I know where I'm getting out."

How does a day trader return 175% in a championship and compound $40,000 into $2 million?

## 2. ORIGIN — THE "Before" (The Relatability Engine)

Martin Schwartz was born in 1945 or 1946. He served in the Marine Corps. He studied finance. He worked at various jobs before becoming a trader.

He was running from the conventional path. He could have worked at a firm. He chose to trade on his own. He moved to California. He traded from his beach house.

## 3. THE FIRE — Failures, Rock-Bottom & Turning Points

### The US Investing Championship (1984)

In 1984, Schwartz entered the US Investing Championship. He returned 175%. He won. The performance attracted attention. Jack Schwager featured him in "Market Wizards."

### From $40,000 to $2 Million

Over 10 years, Schwartz turned $40,000 into over $2 million trading S&P futures. He was a day trader. He did not hold positions overnight. He used technical analysis.

### The Beach House

Schwartz traded from his beach house in California. He did not need a Wall Street office. He needed charts and discipline.

### Market Wizards

Schwartz was featured in "Market Wizards" by Jack Schwager. The book made him famous among traders. His strict discipline and risk management stood out.

## 4. THE GRIND — The Invisible Years (The Method)

Schwartz's method is defined by discipline, cutting losses, and technical analysis.

### Discipline

Schwartz pre-determined his exits. "Before I place a trade, I know where I'm getting out." He did not hold losing positions.

### Technical Analysis

Schwartz used charts, moving averages, and momentum indicators. He did not rely on fundamentals. He relied on price.

### Trend Following

Schwartz's famous principle: "Buy high, sell higher." He did not try to catch bottoms. He rode trends.

### Cutting Losses

Schwartz's famous quote: "Learn to take a loss. Losses are part of the game. If you don't take losses, you won't make money."

The invisible years—the years before the championship—were years of learning, losing, and refining. Schwartz did not start winning immediately. He learned from losses.

## 5. THE TEST — The Breakthrough Moment

The 1984 championship was the test. Schwartz returned 175%. He won. The test was not just profit. The test was discipline.
