# Ed Seykota — The Systematic Trader Who Said "Everyone Gets What They Want"

## 1. THE HOOK

In 1972, he started with $5,000. By 1988, he had turned it into $15 million. That is a 250,000% return over 16 years—an average of over 60% per year. He did not trade discretionarily. He built a computer system that followed rules. He was one of the first to use computers for systematic trading. In the 1970s, when most traders were still using paper charts, Ed Seykota was writing code. He appeared in Jack Schwager's "Market Wizards" and became famous for his philosophy: "Everyone gets what they want from the market." If you want excitement, you get excitement—but you lose money. If you want profits, you follow the rules. He is known for a famous rule: cut losses, cut losses, cut losses.

How does a computer programmer become one of the greatest traders in history?

## 2. ORIGIN — The "Before" (The Relatability Engine)

Ed Seykota was born August 7, 1946, in Portland, Oregon. He studied electrical engineering and computer science at MIT, graduating in 1969. He was trained as an engineer.

In the early 1970s, he worked at IBM. He was interested in markets. He began writing computer programs to test trading systems. He was one of the first to use computers for systematic trading.

He was running from the discretionary approach. Most traders made decisions based on charts, news, and intuition. Seykota wanted rules. He wanted a system that could be tested and followed.

## 3. THE FIRE — Failures, Rock-Bottom & Turning Points

### Systematic Trading (1970s)

In the 1970s, Seykota built one of the first computerized trading systems. He tested trend-following rules on historical data. He found that simple rules—buy when prices go up, sell when prices go down—could make money. The key was not the rules. The key was following them.

### $5,000 to $15 Million

From 1972 to 1988, Seykota turned $5,000 into $15 million. The return was remarkable. He did not have a losing year. He followed his system. He did not deviate.

### Market Wizards (1989)

Jack Schwager interviewed Seykota for "Market Wizards." The interview became one of the most quoted in the book. Seykota's philosophy—that psychology drives trading, and that "everyone gets what they want from the market"—resonated with traders.

### Trading Workshops

Seykota conducted trading workshops. He taught traders about psychology, system design, and discipline. He emphasized that feelings are a real-time indicator of trading issues. If you feel anxious, fearful, or greedy, your system is not aligned with your psychology.

## 4. THE GRIND — The Invisible Years (The Method)

Seykota's method is defined by systematic trading, cutting losses, and psychology.

### Systematic Trading

Seykota built computer systems that follow rules. The rules are simple: buy when prices trend up, sell when prices trend down. The complexity is not in the rules. It is in following them.

### Cutting Losses

Seykota's famous rule: "The elements of good trading are: (1) cutting losses, (2) cutting losses, (3) cutting losses." If you cut losses, you survive. If you do not, you blow up.

### Psychology

Seykota believes that trading is psychological. "Everyone gets what they want from the market." If you want excitement, you get excitement—but you lose money. If you want profits, you follow the rules.

The invisible years—the years before Market Wizards—were years of testing systems. Seykota ran computer simulations. He tested rules on historical data. He built a system that worked.

## 5. THE TEST — The Breakthrough Moment

The track record—$5,000 to $15 million—was the test. Seykota's system worked. He followed it. He did not deviate. The test was not the system. The test was the discipline.

## 7. THE LEGACY — Purpose Beyond Profit + Human Side

Ed Seykota is private. After the Market Wizards interview, he became somewhat reclusive. He teaches workshops.

### What does he regret?

Seykota does not speak extensively about regrets. His track record suggests that his system worked.

### What does he want to be remembered for?

In interviews, Seykota emphasizes psychology. He wants traders to understand that feelings are information. If you feel anxious, your system is not aligned with your beliefs.

Ed Seykota's career demonstrates that trading is 10% system and 90% psychology.
