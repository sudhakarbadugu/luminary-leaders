# Simone Biles

**Seven-Time Olympic Medalist, 32-Time World Championship Medalist, Mental Health Pioneer**

---

## Early Life and Introduction to Gymnastics

Simone Arianne Biles was born on September 14, 1997, in Columbus, Ohio, to mother Shanon Biles who struggled with addiction and was unable to care for her children. Simone and her sister Adria were placed in foster care before being raised by their grandparents Ron and Nellie Biles in Spring, Texas. The security and stability of her grandparents' home provided the foundation for her extraordinary athletic development.

Her natural talent for gymnastics emerged during a daycare field trip to a gymnasium at age six. Within weeks of beginning formal training, her coaches recognized abilities that warranted accelerated development. She trained at World Champions Gymnastics under coaches Amanda and Mark Biles, progressing through competitive levels faster than typical timelines.

She adopted gymnastics as her primary focus around age twelve, when home-schooling allowed intensive training schedules. The decision to pursue elite gymnastics rather than traditional education reflected family support and recognition that she possessed abilities warranting specialized development. Her grandparents made significant sacrifices to support her training and competition schedules.

---

## Dominance at the Elite Level

Biles rose through the USA Gymnastics development program rapidly, earning her first senior national team selection in 2013 at age fifteen. Her debut at the 2013 World Championships announced her arrival, winning gold in floor exercise and bronze in balance beam. The performance demonstrated the extraordinary power and precision that would define her competitive legacy.

Her first Olympic appearance came at Rio 2016, where she won four gold medals—individual all-around, vault, floor exercise, and team competition. She added a bronze on balance beam, establishing herself as the dominant gymnast of her generation. Her performance in Rio exceeded expectations and set the standard for subsequent competitions.

She continued accumulating World Championship medals at an unprecedented rate, accumulating 32 total medals across multiple World Championships. The tally surpasses any gymnast in history, with her seven Olympic medals ranking among the most decorated Olympic gymnasts ever. Her sustained excellence across multiple Olympic cycles demonstrated physical and psychological resilience.

---

## The Yurchenko Double Tuck and Signature Skills

Biles revolutionized gymnastics through her execution of skills that others could not attempt. The Yurchenko double tuck—a round-off onto the vault table followed by two full twists in the tuck position—became known as the Biles I, demonstrating how her combination of power, technique, and courage enabled skills that transformed competitive expectations.

Her floor exercise passes incorporated multiple flipping combinations that judges rated at the highest difficulty levels. The combination of power and precision required training regimens that pushed human performance boundaries. Other elite gymnasts studied her technique, incorporating elements into their own programs while acknowledging her singular abilities.

She accumulated four skills named after her in the Code of Points—the technical document governing competitive gymnastics scoring. gymnasts who attempt Biles-coded skills must demonstrate abilities beyond standard competition, reflecting the extraordinary difficulty of her contributions to the sport's technical evolution.

## Mental Health Advocacy and Tokyo Olympics

At the 2020 Tokyo Olympics (held in 2021 due to pandemic delays), Biles withdrew from multiple events to prioritize her mental health. She cited "the twisties"—a disorientation condition where gymnasts lose spatial awareness during aerial movements—as a dangerous condition that made competing unsafe. Her decision sparked global conversations about athlete mental health and the pressure elite competitors face.

The decision to withdraw was neither impulsive nor easily made. Biles consulted with coaches, teammates, and medical staff before concluding that her safety and performance required stepping back. Her transparency about her struggles challenged the "tough it out" culture that had dominated Olympic competition. She remained supportive of teammates while prioritizing her own wellbeing.

Her withdrawal from events including the individual all-around allowed Sunisa Lee to compete for gold, demonstrating team solidarity over individual glory. Biles received the Olympic Spirit Award for her courageous decision to prioritize mental health over competitive pressure. The response from fans and athletes was overwhelmingly supportive.

---

## Return and Continued Excellence

Biles returned to competition in 2023, demonstrating that her break had restored both physical capabilities and mental clarity. She competed at World Championships in Antwerp, winning gold in all-around, vault, and floor exercise while rebuilding confidence after the Tokyo challenges. The performance proved she could return to elite competition after addressing mental health concerns.

She announced in 2024 that the Paris Olympics would be her final competition, planning retirement afterward to focus on other life priorities. The announcement positioned Paris as a concluding chapter rather than continuation, allowing her to compete without the pressure of future cycles. Her preparation combined physical training with mental conditioning.

Her Paris performance included a silver medal in the individual all-around and a gold in the vault event final—demonstrating that she could compete successfully while maintaining boundaries around her mental health. The balance between competitive excellence and personal wellbeing characterized her final Olympic chapter.

## Business Ventures and Brand Partnerships

Biles accumulated substantial earnings through competition prize money, sponsorships, and endorsements. Her partnership portfolio included Nike, Uber Eats, and numerous other brands seeking association with her athletic excellence and relatable personality. Her marketability extended beyond gymnastics to general sports audiences and lifestyle brands.

She launched a gymnastics equipment line and authored books about her career and mental health journey. Her memoir "Courage to Soar" (co-written with someone else) detailed her childhood, career development, and Olympic experiences. The book became a bestseller among sports autobiographies, appealing to young readers and gymnastics enthusiasts.

She established a production company, making investments in entertainment content featuring gymnastics and athletic training themes. Her entrepreneurial activities reflected a post-gymnastics career plan that extended beyond the sport while leveraging her expertise and reputation.

## Personal Life and Marriage

Biles married Jonathan Owens, an NFL safety, in 2023 in a ceremony attended by family, friends, and fellow athletes. Their relationship began during the COVID-19 pandemic when they connected through a dating app, building connection despite Owens' football schedule and Biles' training demands. Their partnership demonstrated her ability to maintain personal relationships alongside athletic excellence.

Owens played for the Houston Texans during their relationship, requiring Biles to balance gymnastics training with attending games and supporting his career. The relationship withstood public attention and scrutiny, with both maintaining boundaries around private aspects of their marriage. Their collaborative social media presence provided glimpses into their shared life.

She continued living in Texas, maintaining connections to the community where she grew up while traveling extensively for competition and business activities. Her relationship with her grandparents remained central to her identity, frequently crediting their support for enabling her success.

---

## Legacy and Impact on Gymnastics

Biles transformed gymnastics through technical innovation, demonstrating that women's gymnastics could incorporate power and difficulty previously associated with men's gymnastics. Her influence extended to coaching methodologies and training approaches, with her techniques being studied and incorporated into development programs worldwide.

Her mental health advocacy shifted how elite sports organizations approach athlete wellbeing. USA Gymnastics and the International Olympic Committee implemented enhanced mental health support programs following her Tokyo experiences. The conversations she initiated influenced policy changes across multiple sports organizations.

She inspired a generation of young gymnasts, particularly girls from diverse backgrounds who saw themselves represented in the sport's most prominent athlete. Her willingness to be vulnerable about struggles with anxiety and mental health challenges made her relatable beyond traditional sports hero narratives.

Her competitive record—32 World Championship medals, 7 Olympic medals, multiple signature skills—established benchmarks that may never be equaled. Whether through her continued involvement in gymnastics as a mentor and ambassador, her business activities, or her advocacy work, her influence will extend far beyond her competitive career. She demonstrated that excellence and vulnerability could coexist, and that protecting one's mental health was consistent with competitive drive.
