# John "Jack" Bogle — The Father of Indexing Who Democratized Investing

## 1. THE HOOK

In 1976, he launched the world's first index mutual fund. It raised only $11 million. Critics called it "Bogle's Folly." Today, index funds hold trillions of dollars. He founded Vanguard in 1975. He structured it as a mutual company—owned by shareholders, not external owners. He refused to profit personally from Vanguard's success. His net worth at death: approximately $80-100 million—modest compared to financial industry peers. He saved investors trillions of dollars in fees. He is called the "father of indexing." His famous quote: "Don't look for the needle in the haystack. Just buy the haystack."

How does a fired executive revolutionize investing and save trillions for ordinary investors?

## 2. ORIGIN — THE "Before" (The Relatability Engine)

John "Jack" Bogle was born May 8, 1929, in Montclair, New Jersey. He graduated from Princeton in 1951. His senior thesis argued that mutual funds should be run at cost for shareholders.

He was running from conventional financial wisdom. He believed funds should serve shareholders, not extract value from them.

## 3. THE FIRE — Failures, Rock-Bottom & Turning Points

### Wellington Management

Bogle worked at Wellington Management. He rose through the ranks. In 1974, he was fired after a contentious merger went wrong.

### Founding Vanguard (1975)

In 1975, Bogle founded Vanguard. He structured it as a mutual company—owned by fund shareholders, not external owners. Vanguard would operate "at cost."

### First Index Fund (1976)

In 1976, Bogle launched the First Index Investment Trust—the world's first index mutual fund. It raised only $11 million. Critics mocked it.

### The Index vs. Active Debate

Bogle "won" the debate. Over multi-decade periods, index funds outperform the vast majority of actively managed funds. Today, index funds hold trillions.

## 4. THE GRIND — The Invisible Years (The Method)

Bogle's method is defined by costs, indexing, and long-term holding.

### Costs Matter

Bogle's famous principle: "Costs matter enormously." The expense ratio is the primary predictor of returns. Low costs win.

### The Haystack

Bogle's famous quote: "Don't look for the needle in the haystack. Just buy the haystack." Buy the whole market. Do not try to pick winners.

### Long-Term Holding

Bogle's famous quote: "Time is your friend. Impulse is your enemy. Don't just do something, sit there." Buy and hold.

The invisible years—the years after being fired—were years of building Vanguard. Bogle did not give up.

## 5. THE TEST — The Breakthrough Moment

Vanguard's success is the test. From "Bogle's Folly" to trillions in assets. Indexing is now mainstream.

## 6. THE PHILOSOPHY — Stealable Wisdom

**On Indexing:**

## 7. THE LEGACY — Purpose Beyond Profit + Human Side

John "Jack" Bogle died January 16, 2019. He saved investors trillions in fees.

### Integrity

Bogle refused to profit personally from Vanguard's success. He gave away wealth. His net worth was modest compared to peers.

### Books

Bogle wrote "Common Sense on Mutual Funds" (1999) and "The Little Book of Common Sense Investing" (2007).

### What does he want to be remembered for?

In interviews, Bogle emphasized serving ordinary investors. He wanted investors to understand that costs matter.
