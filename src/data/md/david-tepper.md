# David Tepper — The Distressed Debt Master Who Bet on Banks in 2009

## 1. THE HOOK

In 2009, the banking system was collapsing. Bank of America and Citigroup were trading at fractions of their former value. Most investors were selling everything. David Tepper bought. He bet billions that the Federal Reserve and Treasury would save the system. He was right. The banks recovered. He made billions. In 1993, he founded Appaloosa Management. He returned approximately 30% annualized over his career—one of the best long-term track records in hedge fund history. He specialized in distressed debt. He bought bonds of troubled companies when others were fleeing. He called his philosophy: "Don't fight the Fed." When the Fed is supporting the system, do not bet against it.

How does a man who buys troubled debt make 30% annualized for decades?

Tepper's method is defined by distressed debt, contrarianism, and Fed analysis.

### Distressed Debt

Tepper specialized in bonds of troubled companies. He bought when others were selling. He analyzed fundamentals. He bet on recovery.

### Contrarianism

Tepper's principle: "Be greedy when others are fearful and fearful when others are greedy." When everyone is selling, there is value.

### Don't Fight the Fed

Tepper adapted Martin Zweig's maxim: "Don't fight the Fed." When the Federal Reserve is supporting the system, do not bet against it.

The invisible years—the years before 2009—were years of analyzing distressed companies, betting on recovery, and learning when to be contrarian and when to be careful.
