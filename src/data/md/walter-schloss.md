# Walter Schloss — The Value Investor Who Worked Alone With No Computer

## 1. THE HOOK

For 45 years, he worked in a small office. He had no computer. He had no Bloomberg terminal. He had Value Line reports and annual reports. He bought stocks at 60-80% of book value. He held over 100 stocks at a time. He barely talked to management. He made decisions based on financial statements. His returns: approximately 21% annually for over four decades. He worked for Benjamin Graham. He was part of the value investing tradition. But he did not analyze companies deeply. He just bought cheap stocks. He held them. He waited. Walter Schloss proved that you do not need complex analysis to beat the market. You need discipline and patience.

How does an investor with no computer and 100+ stocks return 21% annually?

## 2. ORIGIN — The "Before" (The Relatability Engine)

Walter Schloss was born in 1912. He worked for Benjamin Graham at Graham-Newman Corp. He learned value investing from the master.

He was running from complexity. He did not build complex models. He did not visit companies. He did not talk to CEOs. He bought stocks that were cheap.

Schloss worked for Graham at Graham-Newman. He learned the value approach: buy stocks below intrinsic value. Focus on the balance sheet. Require a margin of safety.

In 1955, Schloss started his own partnership. He worked alone. He had no computer. He used Value Line. He read annual reports. He bought cheap stocks.

### Performance

Schloss returned approximately 21% annually for over 45 years. His partner, his son Edwin Schloss, joined later. The track record is one of the best in history.

### Method

Schloss bought stocks at 60-80% of book value. He held over 100 stocks. He diversified. He barely sold. He waited for the market to recognize value.

## 4. THE GRIND — The Invisible Years (The Method)

Schloss's method is defined by deep value, diversification, and simplicity.

### Deep Value

Schloss bought stocks at 60-80% of book value. He did not buy stocks at fair value. He bought them at a deep discount.

### Diversification

Schloss held over 100 stocks at a time. He did not concentrate. He spread risk. If one stock went to zero, he survived.

### Simplicity

Schloss had no computer. He had Value Line and annual reports. He read financial statements. He did not do complex modeling. "We try to buy stocks cheap... it's not that complicated."

### No Contact with Management

Schloss deliberately avoided talking to management. He believed that management would spin the story. He preferred to read the financial statements and make his own judgment.

The invisible years—the years in the small office—were years of reading annual reports. Schloss did not have screens. He had paper. That was enough.
