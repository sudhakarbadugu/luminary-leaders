# Carl Icahn — The Activist Who Fought Management and Won

## 1. THE HOOK

He is called a "corporate raider." He is called an "activist." He buys stakes in companies. He pressures management. He demands changes. He unlocks value. His famous battles: TWA (he took control, sold assets, company went bankrupt), RJR Nabisco (he fought KKR), Herbalife (he fought Bill Ackman), Apple (he pressured Tim Cook for buybacks). He is known for the "Icahn Lift"—stocks rise when he discloses his stake. He returned 15%+ annualized for decades. His net worth: $15-20 billion. His famous quote: "A lot of people are smarter than I am, but I'm more stubborn."

How does a man who fights management for decades return 15%+ annualized?

## 2. ORIGIN — THE "Before" (The Relatability Engine)

Carl Icahn was born February 16, 1936, in Queens, New York. His father was a cantor. His mother was a schoolteacher. He studied philosophy at Princeton.

He was running from conventional paths. He could have been a doctor or a lawyer. He chose Wall Street.

## 3. THE FIRE — Failures, Rock-Bottom & Turning Points

### Icahn & Co. (1968)

In 1968, Icahn founded Icahn & Co. with $150,000. He started with arbitrage and options. He recognized that stagnant companies with undervalued assets presented opportunities.

### The Corporate Raider Era (1980s)

In the 1980s, Icahn became a corporate raider. He bought stakes in companies. He demanded asset sales, management changes, restructurings.

### TWA (1985-1992)

In 1985, Icahn acquired TWA. He took the company private. He sold valuable assets—the London-Heathrow routes, the JFK terminal. He profited. TWA filed for bankruptcy in 1992.

### Herbalife vs. Ackman (2012-Present)

In 2012, Bill Ackman shorted Herbalife, calling it a "pyramid scheme." Icahn took the long position. He called Ackman a "liar" on CNBC. The stock rose. Icahn profited.

### Icahn Enterprises

Icahn created Icahn Enterprises, a publicly traded holding company. Investors could participate in his activist campaigns.

## 4. THE GRIND — The Invisible Years (The Method)

Icahn's method is defined by activism, persistence, and stubbornness.

### Activism

Icahn buys stakes. He pressures management. He demands changes. He unlocks value.

### The "Icahn Lift"

Stocks rise when Icahn discloses his stake. Markets anticipate his ability to create change.

### Stubbornness

Icahn's famous quote: "A lot of people are smarter than I am, but I'm more stubborn." He outlasts opponents.

The invisible years—the years of proxy fights—are years of persistence. Icahn does not give up.

## 5. THE TEST — The Breakthrough Moment

The track record—15%+ annualized for decades—is the test. Icahn fought management. He won.

## 7. THE LEGACY — Purpose Beyond Profit + Human Side

Carl Icahn is still active. He invests. He fights.

### Philanthropy

Icahn signed The Giving Pledge. He has donated to Mount Sinai Hospital and Princeton University.

### Controversy

Icahn has been criticized for prioritizing short-term extraction over long-term health. TWA is the defining example.

### What does he want to be remembered for?

In interviews, Icahn emphasizes activism. He wants shareholders to fight for their rights.
