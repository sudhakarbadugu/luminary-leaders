# Howard Schultz — The Brooklyn Kid Who Built Starbucks into Global Coffee Culture

## 1. THE HOOK

In 1983, he traveled to Italy. He saw Italian espresso bars. In 1987, he acquired Starbucks. He grew it from 6 stores to 30,000+. He provided healthcare to part-time workers. He created the "third place." He says: "In life, you can blame circumstances for where you are, or you can shape circumstances to be where you want to be."

How does a kid from Brooklyn housing projects build a global coffee empire?

## 2. ORIGIN — THE "Before" (The Relatability Engine)

Howard Schultz was born July 19, 1953, in Brooklyn, New York. He grew up in the Bayview Houses—a public housing project. His father was a truck driver who lost his job. He studied at Northern Michigan University (BA communications 1975).

He was running from poverty. He saw opportunity. He wanted to create belonging.

## 3. THE FIRE — Failures, Rock-Bottom & Turning Points

### Xerox, Hammarplast

Schultz worked at Xerox in sales. Then Hammarplast—a Swedish drip-coffee maker company.

### Join Starbucks (1982)

In 1982, Schultz joined Starbucks as Director of Retail Operations. 6 stores. Coffee bean retailer.

### Italy Trip (1983)

In 1983, Schultz traveled to Milan, Italy. Italian espresso bars. "Third place" concept.

### Il Giornale (1985)

Starbucks owners rejected his vision. Schultz left and started Il Giornale. His own coffee bar.

### Acquire Starbucks (1987)

In 1987, Schultz acquired Starbucks for $3.8M. Merged with Il Giornale.

### CEO (1986-2000)

Schultz grew Starbucks from 6 stores to 3,000+. Global expansion.

### Return as CEO (2008-2017)

In 2008, during the financial crisis, Schultz returned as CEO. Transformation. Stock prices soared.

## 4. THE GRIND — The Invisible Years (The Method)

Schultz's method is defined by third place, employee benefits, and people business.

### Third Place

Schultz's philosophy: "Third place between home and work." Belonging.

### Employee Benefits

Schultz's philosophy: Healthcare for part-time workers. Stock options. Education.

### People Business

Schultz's philosophy: "We're in the people business serving coffee."

The invisible years—the years at Xerox—gave Schultz sales skills. He built Starbucks.

## 5. THE TEST — THE BREAKTHROUGH MOMENT

30,000+ stores is the test. Healthcare for part-time workers. Third place works.

## 6. THE PHILOSOPHY — Stealable Wisdom

## 7. THE LEGACY — Purpose Beyond Profit + Human Side

Howard Schultz gives to philanthropy. He ran for president in 2020.

### What does he want to be remembered for?

In interviews, Schultz emphasizes people and belonging. He wants to create the third place.
