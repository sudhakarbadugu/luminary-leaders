# Barack Obama

**44th President of the United States (2009–2017)**

## Early Political Rise

Barack Obama entered the national spotlight with his 2004 Democratic National Convention keynote address. His 2008 primary campaign against Hillary Clinton was historic — a Black candidate competing seriously for the presidential nomination, fueled by a coalition of young voters, minorities, and independents. He won by framing himself as a unifier during a period of deep partisan division. The primary exposed tensions between Clinton's institutional strength and Obama's message of change.

## The 2008 General Election and Historic Victory

Obama faced Senator John McCain in the general election. His campaign set fundraising records, built a massive small-donor base, and deployed sophisticated data analytics. He won 365 electoral votes to McCain's 173, becoming the first African American president of the United States. His victory speech at Grant Park in Chicago signaled a new era of hope and optimism.

## Domestic Policy Achievements

Obama's first term was defined by the Affordable Care Act, which extended health coverage to millions of uninsured Americans. He signed the Dodd-Frank financial reform act, pushed the Recovery Act to combat the Great Recession, and ended Don't Ask Don't Tell. His administration oversaw the auto industry bailout and pushed for immigration reform via the Dream Act.

## Foreign Policy and National Security

Obama ordered the operation that killed Osama bin Laden in 2011. He pursued a diplomatic opening with Iran that led to the JCPOA nuclear deal, normalized relations with Cuba, and managed the withdrawal of U.S. troops from Iraq. His approach emphasized multilateralism and avoiding large-scale ground wars, though he was criticized for military interventions in Libya and continued drone strikes.

## The 2010 Midterm "Shellacking"

In the 2010 midterms, Democrats lost 63 seats in the House and 6 in the Senate. The Republican Party won the largest House majority since 1947. Obama acknowledged the loss as a "shellacking," driven by high unemployment, voter frustration, and the rise of the Tea Party movement. The results forced him to shift to executive actions and executive orders for the remainder of his presidency.

## 2012 Reelection and Second Term

Obama won a decisive victory against Mitt Romney in 2012, improving his standing among Hispanic and Asian American voters. He pursued executive actions on immigration (DACA/DAPA), signed the Paris Climate Agreement, and championed marriage equality after the Supreme Court decision. He appointed two Supreme Court justices — Sonia Sotomayor and Elena Kagan — the first Latina and the first openly gay woman to serve on the Court.

## 2016 Farewell Address

Obama's Farewell Address in Chicago on January 10, 2017, reflected on progress made and work remaining. He warned against rising nationalism, racial division, and economic inequality. He expressed faith in American democracy and asked citizens to remain engaged. "Being president," he said, "changed my perspective." His approval rating at departure was a historically high 60 percent.

## Legacy and Post-Presidency

Obama launched the Obama Foundation, delivered speeches globally, and partnered with Netflix on documentary content. He remained a central figure in Democratic politics, actively campaigning for candidates and shaping the party's direction. His memoir "A Promised Land" became a bestseller. His presidency set a template for data-driven campaigning, coalition building, and cautious executive governance.
