# Brian Armstrong

**Cofounder and CEO of Coinbase Global Inc.**

## Early Life and Education

Brian Armstrong was born in 1983 in San Jose, California. He studied computer science at Rice University, where he developed an interest in economics and monetary policy. After graduating, he worked as a software engineer at various startups, including an internship at the FBI doing data analysis. He grew fascinated with Bitcoin around 2010 and began exploring how digital currencies could reshape the financial system.

## Founding of Coinbase

Armstrong co-founded Coinbase in 2012 with Fred Ehrsam, initially operating from a small office in San Francisco. Coinbase launched as a Bitcoin brokerage and quickly became one of the largest cryptocurrency exchanges in the United States. His vision was to create an open financial system for the internet — giving people around the world access to financial services regardless of their location or status.

## Scaling Coinbase

Under Armstrong's leadership, Coinbase grew from a small startup to a publicly traded company (COIN) on the Nasdaq in April 2021. The direct listing marked a watershed moment for the crypto industry, validating years of regulatory struggle. Coinbase grew to serve over 100 million verified users, holding hundreds of billions of dollars in assets. The company's infrastructure supported dozens of cryptocurrencies and expanded into NFT trading and institutional custody services.

## Balancing Regulation and Innovation

Armstrong navigated complex and evolving regulatory landscapes in the United States and globally. He testified before Congress multiple times, advocated for clear cryptocurrency regulation, and built a compliance-first culture within Coinbase. He faced pressure to delist certain assets and resisted political pressure from both parties. In 2023, the SEC sued Coinbase, alleging securities law violations — a case that became a central battleground for crypto regulation.

## Corporate Culture and "Mission Mode"

Armstrong implemented a corporate philosophy called "Mission Mode," modeled after wartime footing. Employees were asked to work long hours with elevated intensity to serve customers and push the crypto movement forward. He also established Coinbase as a remote-first company, hiring globally. He implemented a controversial policy requiring employees to avoid political activism at work, which drew criticism but also praise for keeping the company focused on its mission.

## Venture Investing and Philanthropy

Armstrong became an active angel investor, backing blockchain startups, fintech companies, and space-related ventures. He co-founded the venture fund Reflexive Capital. Alongside his wife, he launched the Project Empathy foundation, focused on economic empowerment and reducing global poverty. He donated significant sums to covid relief and scientific research, including funding Alzheimer's disease research.

## Personal Philosophy and Public Presence

Armstrong described himself as a "late convert to Christianity" and wrote publicly about his faith. He believed crypto could bring economic freedom to billions, particularly in countries with hyperinflation or authoritarian regimes. He frequently posted on social media, writing long reflections on crypto economics, the future of money, and the responsibilities of builders in the space.

## Net Worth and Recognition

Armstrong's net worth peaked at over $10 billion during the 2021 crypto boom. Forbes included him on its billionaires list. He appeared on the cover of Fortune magazine and was named one of the most influential people in the crypto industry. Despite the volatility of crypto markets and regulatory uncertainty, Armstrong remained one of the industry's most recognizable advocates and builders.
