# Henry Kravis — The King of Private Equity Who Won RJR Nabisco

## 1. THE HOOK

In 1988, he won the largest leveraged buyout in history. RJR Nabisco. $25 billion. The deal was documented in "Barbarians at the Gate." He fought Ross Johnson, Ted Forstmann, and other bidders. He won. He is called the "King of Private Equity." He cofounded KKR in 1976 with his cousin George Roberts and mentor Jerome Kohlberg. KKR manages over $500 billion today. His net worth: $6-7 billion. His approach: "Buy, improve, sell." He pioneered the LBO industry. His famous quote: "A real entrepreneur is somebody who has no safety net underneath them."

How does a man from Tulsa, Oklahoma, become the king of private equity?

## 2. ORIGIN — THE "Before" (The Relatability Engine)

Henry Kravis was born January 6, 1944, in Tulsa, Oklahoma. He studied at Claremont McKenna College and Columbia Business School. He started at Bear Stearns.

He was running from conventional careers. He cofounded KKR in 1976. He had no safety net.

## 3. THE FIRE — Failures, Rock-Bottom & Turning Points

### Founding KKR (1976)

In 1976, Kravis cofounded KKR with George Roberts (cousin) and Jerome Kohlberg (mentor). They pioneered the LBO industry.

### RJR Nabisco (1988)

In 1988, KKR won the RJR Nabisco deal. It was the largest LBO in history—$25 billion. The deal was documented in "Barbarians at the Gate." Kravis fought Ross Johnson and other bidders. He won.

### KKR Today

KKR manages over $500 billion in assets. It is one of the largest private equity firms globally.

### Net Worth

Kravis's net worth is estimated at $6-7 billion.

## 4. THE GRIND — The Invisible Years (The Method)

Kravis's method is defined by leverage, operational improvements, and value creation.

### The Formula

Kravis's approach: "Buy, improve, sell." Use leverage to acquire. Improve operations. Sell at a premium.

### Operational Improvements

Kravis emphasized operational improvements. "We create value by improving companies."

### Leverage

KKR uses debt financing to acquire companies. The leverage amplifies returns.

The invisible years—the years of building KKR—were years of demonstrating that LBOs could create real value.

## 5. THE TEST — The Breakthrough Moment

The RJR Nabisco deal was the test. Kravis won the largest LBO in history. He proved that private equity could handle mega-deals.

## 6. THE PHILOSOPHY — Stealable Wisdom

## 7. THE LEGACY — Purpose Beyond Profit + Human Side

Henry Kravis is still active. He runs KKR. He is a philanthropist.

### Philanthropy

Kravis donated to the Kravis Center for Performing Arts, Mount Sinai Hospital, and Claremont McKenna College. He established the Kravis Prize for Leadership.

### What does he want to be remembered for?

In interviews, Kravis emphasizes value creation and entrepreneurship. He wants investors to improve companies.
