# Vinod Khosla — The Contrarian Who Invests in Science Experiments

## 1. THE HOOK

In 1982, he cofounded Sun Microsystems. He was CEO until 1984. He joined Kleiner Perkins in 1986. In 2004, he founded Khosla Ventures. He invests in "black swan" technologies. He says: "We invest in science experiments." His investments: Juniper Networks, Google, Square, Stripe, DoorDash, Instacart, Impossible Foods. His net worth: $6-7 billion. His famous quote: "Failure is not an option, it's a requirement." He predicts: "20 years from now we'll have reinvented everything."

How does an immigrant from India become one of Silicon Valley's most contrarian investors?

## 2. ORIGIN — THE "Before" (The Relatability Engine)

Vinod Khosla was born January 28, 1955, in New Delhi, India. He studied at IIT Delhi. He came to the US for graduate school.

He was running from conventional careers. He saw technology as the way to change the world.

## 3. THE FIRE — Failures, Rock-Bottom & Turning Points

### Sun Microsystems (1982-1985)

In 1982, Khosla cofounded Sun Microsystems with Andy Bechtolsheim, Bill Joy, and Scott McNealy. He was CEO until 1984. He left in 1985.

### Kleiner Perkins (1986-2004)

In 1986, Khosla joined Kleiner Perkins as a general partner. He built his reputation.

### Khosla Ventures (2004-Present)

In 2004, Khosla founded Khosla Ventures. He pursued his distinctive investment philosophy.

### Investments

Khosla invested in Juniper Networks, Google, Square, Stripe, DoorDash, Instacart, Impossible Foods.

### Clean Tech

Khosla invested heavily in clean tech and green energy. Some failed. Some succeeded. He accepted failure as part of innovation.

## 4. THE GRIND — The Invisible Years (The Method)

Khosla's method is defined by breakthrough thinking, accepting failure, and contrarian bets.

### Breakthrough Change

Khosla's philosophy: "Most people think in terms of incremental improvement. I think in terms of breakthrough change."

### Invest in Science Experiments

Khosla backs breakthrough scientific innovations. He does not seek proven business models.

### Accept Failure

Khosla's famous quote: "Failure is not an option, it's a requirement." He accepts that many investments will fail.

The invisible years—the years of clean tech investments—were years of learning from failures. Khosla did not fear being wrong.

## 5. THE TEST — The Breakthrough Moment

The investments—Google, Square, Stripe—are the test. Khosla identified transformative companies.
