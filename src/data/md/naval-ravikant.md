# Naval Ravikant

## Early Life and Education
Naval Ravikant was born on April 17, 1974, in Delhi, India, to a middle-class family. His father was an accountant, and his mother worked as a teacher. The family emigrated to the United States when Naval was seven, settling in New York and later moving to Queens. He attended Stuyvesant High School before studying at the City College of New York, where he earned a degree in Computer Science. Naval briefly attended Stanford for graduate studies but dropped out to pursue entrepreneurship, rejecting the conventional path in favor of building something new.

## Technology Career
Naval began his career in the dot-com boom of the 1990s, working as a software engineer at various startups before joining a venture capital firm. He co-founded two companies: Epinions (an early consumer review site) and Weblogs (a blog publishing network acquired by AOL). These early ventures gave him firsthand experience in building and selling technology businesses. Through Epinions, he discovered his talent for aggregating independent voices and creating platforms that empowered creators and consumers alike.

## AngelList and Startup Ecosystem
In 2010, Naval co-founded AngelList, a platform that connects angel investors with early-stage startups. The platform revolutionized startup fundraising by making it accessible to smaller investors and enabling founders to raise capital more efficiently. AngelList grew into the leading platform for early-stage investing, handling billions in fundraising and launching innovations like SPVs (special purpose vehicles) and rolled-up fund structures. Naval remained CEO until 2020, building AngelList into infrastructure that supports the entire startup ecosystem.

## Investment Philosophy
Naval became one of the most successful angel investors of the past two decades. He invested early in companies like Twitter, Uber, FanDuel, Yammer, and Stack Overflow, accumulating stakes worth billions. His investment philosophy emphasizes investing in startups with network effects, brands, or unfair advantages that compound over time. He famously advocates for specific knowledge (deep expertise in niche areas), accountability, and leverage (code and media as scalable tools). Naval's sayings about wealth creation—"learn to sell, learn to build"—have become startup gospel.

## The Naval Podcast and "How to Get Rich"
Beginning in 2017, Naval launched the podcast "The Navalmanack" and later "The Naval Podcast," articulating his philosophy on wealth, happiness, and meaning. He compiled his insights into "The Almanack of Naval Ravikant," distilling complex ideas about venture capital, decision-making, and personal development into accessible wisdom. His tweet storm on "How to Get Rich" went viral, generating millions of impressions and spawning an entire genre of productivity and wealth creation content.

## Personal Health and Philosophy
Naval has been remarkably transparent about his struggles with health and happiness. He has discussed dealing with chronic pain, the death of his father, and periods of depression. This transparency endeared him to audiences seeking authentic wisdom rather than polished success stories. He practices meditation and has explored philosophy extensively, drawing from Stoicism, Buddhism, and evolutionary psychology. His ideas on health—prioritizing sleep, exercise, and nutrition—reflect a systems-thinking approach to personal optimization.

## Influence on Startup Culture
Naval reshaped how entrepreneurs think about fundraising, company building, and personal development. His framework for evaluating opportunities, negotiating deals, and thinking long-term has influenced a generation of founders. He became a sought-after voice on Twitter (now X), where his original observations and replies attracted millions of followers. Unlike many public figures, Naval avoided building a personal brand for monetization—he simply shared what he believed to be true, trusting that authenticity would resonate.

## Legacy and Current Activities
Naval remains active in the startup ecosystem while guarding his privacy more than in previous years. He focuses on reading, thinking, and occasional angel investments rather than operational roles. His greatest legacy may be democratizing startup investing and articulating a coherent philosophy for building wealth and living well. In a technology world full of hype, Naval's emphasis on substance, rationality, and intellectual honesty stands out as a lasting contribution to startup culture.
