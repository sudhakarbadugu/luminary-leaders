# John Templeton — The Contrarian Who Bought at Maximum Pessimism

## 1. THE HOOK

In 1939, World War II had just begun. Europe was in chaos. Investors were selling everything. John Templeton bought. He purchased 104 stocks on the New York Stock Exchange. All were trading below $1. Four years later, he had made multiple times his money. It was his first contrarian trade. In 1954, he founded the Templeton Growth Fund. It returned approximately 15% annually for 38 years. It was one of the first global mutual funds. He invested in Japan in the 1950s when few Americans did. He avoided the dot-com bubble in 1999-2000. He sold his funds to Franklin Resources in 1992 for $440 million. He gave away hundreds of millions to philanthropy. He is famous for one quote: "The four most dangerous words in investing are: this time it's different."

How does a man who bought in 1939 and avoided the dot-com bubble stay contrarian for seven decades?

## 2. ORIGIN — THE "Before" (The Relatability Engine)

John Templeton was born November 29, 1912, in Tennessee. His family was not wealthy. He studied at Yale and then Oxford as a Rhodes Scholar.

He was running from conventional thinking. Most Americans invested only in American stocks. Templeton looked globally. He asked: where is the value?

## 3. THE FIRE — Failures, Rock-Bottom & Turning Points

### The 1939 Trade

In 1939, World War II began. Investors were selling. Templeton bought 104 stocks trading below $1. He borrowed money to do it. Four years later, he had multiplied his investment. It was his first contrarian trade.

### Templeton Growth Fund (1954)

In 1954, Templeton founded the Templeton Growth Fund. It was one of the first global mutual funds. Most Americans invested only in US stocks. Templeton invested globally.

### Japan in the 1950s

In the 1950s, Templeton invested in Japan. Few Americans did. Japan was recovering from World War II. Templeton saw growth. The investment paid off.

### Avoiding the Dot-Com Bubble (1999-2000)

In 1999-2000, technology stocks were soaring. Templeton avoided them. He saw euphoria. He said "this time it's different" are the four most dangerous words. The bubble burst. He was right.

### Selling the Funds (1992)

In 1992, Templeton sold his funds to Franklin Resources for $440 million. He focused on philanthropy.

## 4. THE GRIND — The Invisible Years (The Method)

Templeton's method is defined by contrarianism, global diversification, and humility.

### Contrarianism

Templeton's famous principle: "Buy at the point of maximum pessimism." When everyone is selling, buy. When everyone is buying, sell.

### Global Diversification

Templeton was one of the first to invest globally. He looked for value anywhere. He did not limit himself to the US.

### Humility

Templeton's other famous principle: "The four most dangerous words in investing are: this time it's different." Markets cycle. Euphoria ends. Be humble.

The invisible years—the years before Templeton Growth Fund—were years of traveling, learning, and finding value where others did not.

## 5. THE TEST — The Breakthrough Moment

The 1939 trade was the test. Europe was at war. Investors were selling. Templeton bought. The trade was contrarian. It was right.

## 6. THE PHILOSOPHY — Stealable Wisdom

**On Contrarianism:**
"Buy at the point of maximum pessimism." When everyone is selling, there is value. When everyone is buying, there is risk.

**On Global Investing:**
Templeton looked for value anywhere. He did not limit himself to the US. Japan in the 1950s. Emerging markets. Anywhere there is pessimism and value.

**On Humility:**
"The four most dangerous words in investing are: this time it's different." Markets cycle. Do not believe that bubbles will last.

**Key Quotes:**
- "Bull markets are born on pessimism, grow on skepticism, mature on optimism, and die on euphoria."
- "The four most dangerous words in investing are: this time it's different."

## 7. THE LEGACY — Purpose Beyond Profit + Human Side

John Templeton died July 8, 2008. He gave away hundreds of millions.

### Philanthropy

Templeton created the Templeton Foundation. It funds research on science, religion, and character development. The Templeton Prize honors progress in spiritual realities.

### Net Worth

Templeton's net worth was estimated at billions. He gave away most of it.

### What does he want to be remembered for?

In interviews, Templeton emphasized contrarianism and humility. He wanted investors to look where others do not.
