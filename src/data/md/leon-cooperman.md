# Leon Cooperman — The Bronx Value Investor Who Tells It Like It Is

## 1. THE HOOK

He spent 25 years at Goldman Sachs. He became a partner and chairman of Goldman Sachs Asset Management. In 1991, he retired and founded Omega Advisors. He returned approximately 12-15% annualized over his career. He is known for quarterly letters—widely read on Wall Street. He is known for candid commentary. He says: "I'm from the Bronx, I tell it like it is." He signed the Giving Pledge in 2010. He converted Omega to a family office in 2018. His net worth: $2-3 billion. His famous quote: "The stock market is a device for transferring money from the impatient to the patient."

How does a kid from the Bronx become a billionaire value investor?

## 2. ORIGIN — THE "Before" (The Relatability Engine)

Leon Cooperman was born April 25, 1943, in the Bronx, New York. He is the son of Polish-Jewish immigrants.

He was running from poverty. He worked hard. He climbed the ladder.

## 3. THE FIRE — Failures, Rock-Bottom & Turning Points

### Goldman Sachs (1966-1991)

Cooperman spent 25 years at Goldman Sachs. He became a partner. He became chairman of Goldman Sachs Asset Management.

### Omega Advisors (1991-2018)

In 1991, Cooperman retired from Goldman Sachs and founded Omega Advisors. He returned approximately 12-15% annualized.

### Family Office (2018)

In 2018, Cooperman converted Omega from a partnership to a family office. He returned outside capital.

### SEC Investigation (2016)

In 2016, Cooperman faced an insider trading investigation. He settled with the SEC without admitting wrongdoing. He paid a civil fine.

## 4. THE GRIND — The Invisible Years (The Method)

Cooperman's method is defined by value investing, dividends, and patience.

### Value Investing

Cooperman is a value investor. He buys undervalued stocks. "Price is what you pay, value is what you get."

### Dividends

Cooperman emphasizes dividend-paying stocks. They provide income and margin of safety.

### Patience

Cooperman's famous quote: "The stock market is a device for transferring money from the impatient to the patient."

The invisible years—the years at Goldman Sachs—were years of learning. Cooperman developed his approach.

## 5. THE TEST — The Breakthrough Moment

The long-term track record—12-15% annualized—is the test. Cooperman built wealth through value investing.
