# Paul Tudor Jones — The Macro Trader Who Prepared for the Crash

## 1. THE HOOK

On October 19, 1987—Black Monday—the Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 22% in a single day. It was the largest one-day percentage decline in history. Most investors lost fortunes. Paul Tudor Jones made approximately $100 million. He had shorted stock index futures. He had seen it coming—not by predicting, but by preparing. He studied market history. He compared 1987 to 1929. He noticed patterns. When the crash came, he was ready. The trade made him famous. It also established his reputation as one of the greatest macro traders of his generation.

How does a trader make $100 million in a day when others lose everything?

## 2. ORIGIN — The "Before" (The Relatability Engine)

Paul Tudor Jones II was born September 28, 1954, in Memphis, Tennessee. His family was middle class. His uncle, Billy Dunavant, was a successful cotton trader. Jones was introduced to commodity trading early.

He attended the University of Virginia, where he studied economics and boxed. He graduated in 1976. He was interested in trading, not investment banking. He went to the New York Cotton Exchange.

He was running from the conventional path. He could have gone to business school or taken a job on Wall Street. He chose commodities—rougher, more volatile, less respected.

## 3. THE FIRE — Failures, Rock-Bottom & Turning Points

### Learning to Trade

Jones's early years were not smooth. He worked at the New York Cotton Exchange and at E.F. Hutton. He made mistakes. He blew out an account early—losing all his money. He learned a lesson: cut losses. Never let a small loss become a large one.

### Tudor Investment Corporation (1980)

In 1980, Jones founded Tudor Investment Corporation. The fund was a macro fund—it traded stocks, bonds, currencies, and commodities based on global economic themes. It was not a hedge fund that picked individual stocks. It was a fund that traded markets.

### Black Monday (1987)

The trade that made Jones famous was his short position before Black Monday. He had studied market history. He noticed that 1987 looked like 1929. Market breadth was deteriorating. Prices were rising on fewer stocks. Volatility was increasing. He bought protection.

When the market crashed, his fund was up approximately 200% for the year. Most investors were down. The trade established Jones as one of the best macro traders in the world.

### Robin Hood Foundation (1988)

In 1988, Jones founded the Robin Hood Foundation, a charity that fights poverty in New York City. The foundation applies the investor mindset to philanthropy—measuring outcomes, demanding accountability, and funding programs that work. Jones has donated over $1 billion personally.

## 4. THE GRIND — The Invisible Years (The Method)

Jones's method is defined by preparation, risk management, and psychology.

### Preparation

Jones does not predict. He prepares. He studies market history. He studies economic data. He builds scenarios. When the market moves, he is ready.

### Risk Management

Jones cuts losses. He has said that "losers average losers"—meaning that adding to a losing position is a mistake. If a trade is wrong, exit. Do not compound the error.

### Psychology

Jones believes that trading is mostly psychology. Markets are driven by fear and greed. If you can control your emotions, you can exploit the emotions of others.

The invisible years—the years before Black Monday—were years of preparation. Jones did not predict the crash. He prepared for multiple scenarios. When the crash came, he was ready.

## 5. THE TEST — The Breakthrough Moment

Black Monday was the test. Jones was short. The market crashed. He made a fortune while others lost everything. The trade was not luck. It was preparation combined with conviction.

## 6. THE PHILOSOPHY — Stealable Wisdom

**On Preparation:**

**On Risk Management:**

**On Psychology:**
Markets are driven by fear and greed. If you can control your emotions, you can exploit the emotions of others.

## 7. THE LEGACY — Purpose Beyond Profit + Human Side

Paul Tudor Jones is still active. Tudor Investment Corporation manages billions in assets.

### Philanthropy

Jones has donated over $1 billion to the Robin Hood Foundation. The foundation has distributed billions to fight poverty in New York City.

### Personal Life

Jones is married and has children. He is known for being private about his personal life and public about his philanthropy.

### What does he regret?

Jones has said that he regrets blowing out his account early in his career. The lesson—cut losses—has stayed with him.

### What does he want to be remembered for?

In interviews, Jones has said he wants to be remembered for his philanthropy as much as his trading. The Robin Hood Foundation is his legacy.
