# Brian Chesky

**Cofounder and CEO of Airbnb, Inc.**

## Early Life and Education

Brian Chesky was born in 1981 in Los Altos, California. He grew up with a deep appreciation for design and architecture, influenced by his mother who worked as a real estate agent. Chesky attended the Rhode Island School of Design (RISD), where he studied industrial design. He met Joe Gebbia during a class project, and the two became close friends. After graduating, Chesky worked as a brand designer at a small agency and later as an intern at Facebook when it was still a startup.

## Birth of Airbnb

In 2007, Chesky and Gebbia could not afford rent in San Francisco. They decided to rent out their apartment's air mattresses to conference attendees, offering breakfast. They called the service "Air Bed and Breakfast." The idea was born out of necessity but quickly evolved into a platform that would transform global travel. They brought in Nathan Blecharczyk as a technical cofounder to build the web infrastructure. The early team pitched investors with a cereal box branding campaign during the 2008 Democratic National Convention.

## Growth and Scaling

Airbnb grew from a niche service for budget travelers to a global platform with millions of listings in nearly every country. Chesky focused on design excellence, personally reviewing every new product feature. He navigated trust and safety challenges, built a 24/7 customer support operation, and developed the company's iconic brand identity. Airbnb expanded beyond accommodation to include experiences and restaurants. By 2020, the company had hosted over 800 million check-ins and built a community of hosts earning billions in supplemental income.

## COVID-19 Pandemic and IPO

The pandemic nearly destroyed Airbnb. Bookings collapsed by 80 percent in spring 2020, and the company laid off 25 percent of its workforce. Chesky cut costs, raised emergency funding, and refocused on core home-sharing. He redesigned the app, built new cleaning protocols, and pivoted to local and long-term stays. Airbnb went public in December 2020 via a direct listing on the Nasdaq. The stock surged, valuing the company at over $100 billion and making Chesky a billionaire many times over.

## Design-Centered Leadership

Chesky was known for leading through design, treating Airbnb as a consumer product company above all else. He personally interviewed every new employee for years and maintained an obsessive focus on the host and guest experience. He authored a set of design principles called "12 Believes" that guided product decisions. He credited his art school background with teaching him to think visually and to solve human problems through empathy and craft.

## Brand and Trust Building

After a series of high-profile incidents involving property damage and safety issues, Chesky invested heavily in trust infrastructure. Airbnb introduced background checks, $1 million host guarantee, and 24/7 customer support. Chesky personally wrote letters to hosts and guests, cultivating a sense of community. He built the brand around belonging and the idea that people could feel at home anywhere in the world. He partnered with cities to collect and remit hotel taxes and complied with local regulations globally.

## Philanthropy and Airbnb.org

Chesky launched Airbnb.org, a nonprofit arm that provided free or subsidized housing for disaster relief and refugees. During the 2022 Ukraine crisis, Airbnb.org housed over 100,000 refugees. He donated millions to various causes and committed to being a "mission-driven" company rather than purely profit-focused. He and Gebbia signed the Giving Pledge, committing to give away the majority of their wealth during their lifetimes.

## Net Worth and Recognition

Chesky's net worth exceeded $20 billion following the IPO, making him one of the wealthiest people in the travel and technology sectors. Fortune named him one of the world's greatest leaders. He appeared on the cover of Time magazine and was featured in documentaries about the sharing economy. He remained one of the most prominent CEOs to emerge from the Silicon Valley startup ecosystem of the late 2000s.
