# Bill Dunn — The Trend Follower Who Stayed When Others Quit

## 1. THE HOOK

He has run a trend-following strategy since 1974—almost fifty years. In that time, he has endured drawdowns of 30%, 40%, even 50%. He has watched his assets under management shrink dramatically. He has seen years when his strategy lost money while others made money. And he has never changed the core of what he does. Bill Dunn did not invent trend following. He did not coin the term. But he stayed with it longer than almost anyone else. When others quit after drawdowns, Dunn held. And over decades, trend following delivered.

How does a man stick with a strategy through 50% drawdowns for fifty years?

## 2. ORIGIN — The "Before" (The Relatability Engine)

Bill Dunn founded Dunn Capital Management in the early 1980s. But his track record as a Commodity Trading Advisor (CTA) began in 1974, making him one of the longest continuously operating trend followers in the world.

He graduated from the University of Colorado with a degree in physics. He was trained as a scientist. The scientific method—hypothesis, test, evidence—shaped his approach to markets.

He was running from the expectation that successful trading requires constant adjustment. The conventional wisdom was that markets change, and strategies must change with them. Dunn believed that some principles endure.

## 3. THE FIRE — Failures, Rock-Bottom & Turning Points

### The 1980s

Dunn began trading in the 1970s, a decade of inflation, oil shocks, and market volatility. Trend following—buying assets that are rising and selling assets that are falling—worked. In the 1980s, Dunn's strategy generated strong returns. Assets under management grew.

### The 1990s

The 1990s were a period of strong performance for many trend followers. The Asian financial crisis, the Russian default, and other market dislocations created trends that systematic strategies could capture. Dunn's fund attracted capital.

### The 1999-2000 Drawdown

In 1999 and 2000, trend following struggled. Markets were choppy—trends did not develop or were reversed quickly. Dunn's fund experienced a significant drawdown. Assets under management declined. Many investors withdrew.

But Dunn did not change his strategy. He believed that trend following would work again. It did. In the early 2000s, trends reappeared. The fund recovered and reached new highs.

### The 2013-2015 Period

In the mid-2010s, trend following again struggled. Central bank intervention reduced volatility. Markets became mean-reverting rather than trending. Dunn's fund experienced another drawdown.

Again, Dunn did not change the core of his strategy. He adjusted parameters. He refined risk management. But he did not abandon trend following.

### The Recovery

Trend following recovered after 2015. Dunn's fund reached new equity highs. Decades of returns compounded. Dunn became known as one of the longest-tenured trend followers in the world.

## 4. THE GRIND — The Invisible Years (The Method)

Dunn's method is defined by systematic rules, trend following, and discipline through drawdowns.

### Systematic Rules

Dunn's strategy is systematic. It is not discretionary. The rules determine when to buy, when to sell, how much to risk. Human judgment is removed from daily decisions.

### Trend Following

Trend following does not predict. It reacts. When prices rise, the strategy buys. When prices fall, the strategy sells. It does not forecast why prices are moving. It follows.

### Discipline Through Drawdowns

Trend following has long periods of underperformance. The strategy can lose money for months or years. Dunn's discipline is not to quit during those periods. He has said that "trend following works until it doesn't, and then it works again."

### Long-Term View

Dunn's time horizon is decades, not months. He understands that a strategy can underperform for years and still deliver excellent long-term returns. Patience is not just a virtue—it is the strategy.

The invisible years—the years during drawdowns—were years of holding. Many trend followers quit. Many investors withdrew. Dunn held.

## 5. THE TEST — The Breakthrough Moment

Every drawdown is the test. The question is: will the strategy survive until trends reappear? Dunn's willingness to stay with his strategy through multiple drawdowns is his defining characteristic.

## 6. THE PHILOSOPHY — Stealable Wisdom

**On Trend Following:**
"Trend following works until it doesn't, and then it works again." Markets trend and mean-revert in cycles. A trend-following strategy will underperform during mean-reverting periods. But eventually, trends will emerge again.

**On Drawdowns:**
Drawdowns are the cost of admission for trend following. If you cannot tolerate 30-50% drawdowns, you cannot capture the long-term returns.

**On Discipline:**
Most traders quit after large drawdowns. Dunn did not. If you change your strategy during a drawdown, you sell low and buy high. You abandon a strategy precisely when it is about to recover.

**Key Quotes:**
- "Trend following works until it doesn't, and then it works again."
- "We don't predict. We follow."

## 7. THE LEGACY — Purpose Beyond Profit + Human Side

Bill Dunn is still active. He is one of the longest-tenured Commodity Trading Advisors in the world.

### Longevity

Most CTAs do not survive for fifty years. They blow up. They quit. They change strategies. Dunn has survived. His longevity is his legacy.

### Influence

Dunn, along with John W. Henry, Bill Eckhardt, and others, is considered a "Godfather of Managed Futures." He demonstrated that systematic trend following can work over decades—if you can stick with it.

### What does he regret?

Dunn has not spoken extensively about regrets. His career is defined by not quitting. If he had regrets, they might be about times when he was too early or too late. But regret is not the lesson. Discipline is.

### What does he want to be remembered for?

In interviews, Dunn has said he wants to be remembered for demonstrating that systematic trend following can work over the long term. It is not easy, but it is possible.

## 8. FINAL MOTIVATION & PERSONAL APPLICATION Guide

Bill Dunn's career demonstrates that the most important skill in investing is not intelligence, but discipline. He stuck with a strategy for fifty years.

**5 Actionable Steps This Week:**

1. **Define your strategy before you need it** — Dunn's rules are systematic. They are defined before he trades. If you do not have rules, you will make decisions under stress, and you will make bad decisions.

2. **Accept drawdowns as the cost of admission** — Every strategy has periods of underperformance. If you cannot tolerate drawdowns, you cannot capture the long-term returns.

3. **Do not change your strategy during a drawdown** — This is when most traders quit. If you change during a drawdown, you sell low.

4. **Think in decades, not months** — Dunn's track record spans fifty years. If your time horizon is months, you will quit before the strategy recovers.

5. **Trend following is not prediction** — Dunn does not predict. He follows. If you are trying to predict the future, you are doing something different.

**The Hard Question:**

If Bill Dunn could sit through a 50% drawdown and not change his strategy because he knew it would recover—what drawdown are you unable to tolerate that is keeping you from long-term returns?

The hardest part is not the strategy. The hardest part is staying.
