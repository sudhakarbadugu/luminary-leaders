# Marc Andreessen — The Man Who Said "Software Eats the World"

## 1. THE HOOK

In 1993, as an undergraduate at the University of Illinois, he co-created Mosaic—the first web browser with a graphical user interface. It made the internet accessible to non-technical users. In 1994, he cofounded Netscape with Jim Clark. It went public in 1995. It was acquired by AOL in 1999 for $4.2 billion. In 2009, he cofounded Andreessen Horowitz (a16z) with Ben Horowitz. Today, a16z manages over $35 billion. He was an early investor in Facebook, Twitter, Airbnb, Lyft, Slack, Stripe. He wrote the essay "Software Eats the World" in 2011. Every industry will be disrupted by software. He is Marc Andreessen.

How does a kid from Iowa create the modern web and become one of the most influential VCs in history?

## 2. ORIGIN — THE "Before" (The Relatability Engine)

Marc Andreessen was born July 9, 1971, in Cedar Falls, Iowa. He studied computer science at the University of Illinois.

He was running from rural Iowa. He saw the internet before others. He understood its potential.

## 3. THE FIRE — Failures, Rock-Bottom & Turning Points

### Mosaic (1993)

While still an undergraduate, Andreessen co-created Mosaic. It was the first web browser with a graphical user interface. It made the internet accessible to everyone.

### Netscape (1994-1999)

In 1994, Andreessen cofounded Netscape with Jim Clark. It went public in 1995. It sparked the first browser war with Microsoft. It was acquired by AOL in 1999 for $4.2 billion.

### Loudcloud/Opsware (1999-2007)

Andreessen cofounded Loudcloud with Ben Horowitz. It survived the dot-com crash. It pivoted to Opsware. It was sold to HP in 2007 for $1.6 billion.

### Andreessen Horowitz (2009-Present)

In 2009, Andreessen cofounded a16z with Ben Horowitz. The firm manages over $35 billion. It is one of Silicon Valley's most influential VC firms.

### Early Investments

Andreessen was an early investor in Facebook, Twitter, Airbnb, Lyft, Slack, Stripe, Skype, Zoom, Coinbase, GitHub, Pinterest.

## 4. THE GRIND — The Invisible Years (The Method)

Andreessen's method is defined by thesis-driven investing, backing founding CEOs, and software.

### "Software Eats the World"

Andreessen's thesis: every industry will be disrupted by software. He wrote the essay in 2011. He predicted the transformation of healthcare, education, finance, and transportation.

### Thesis-Driven Investing

Andreessen invests based on macro technology trends. He does not chase momentum. He identifies shifts before they happen.

### Backing Founding CEOs

Andreessen believes founders make the best long-term leaders. He backs them. He supports them.

The invisible years—the years of building Netscape, Loudcloud, Opsware—were years of learning. Andreessen survived the dot-com crash. He understood resilience.

## 5. THE TEST — The Breakthrough Moment

The investments—Facebook, Twitter, Airbnb, Stripe—are the test. Andreessen identified the winners early.

## 6. THE PHILOSOPHY — Stealable Wisdom

**On Innovation:**
"Innovation is the ability to see change as an opportunity — not a threat."

**On Software:**
"Software is eating the world."

**On Jobs:**
"The spread of computers and the internet will put jobs in two categories: people who tell computers what to do, and people who are told by computers what to do."

## 7. THE LEGACY — Purpose Beyond Profit + Human Side

Marc Andreessen is still active. He runs a16z. He invests. He writes.

### What does he want to be remembered for?

In interviews, Andreessen emphasizes software, innovation, and opportunity. He wants investors to see change as opportunity.
