# David Geffen

**Founder of DreamWorks Animation, Geffen Records, and Asylum Records**

## Early Life in Brooklyn

David Geffen was born in 1943 in Brooklyn, New York, to a working-class Jewish family. He grew up in the borough, displaying entrepreneurial instincts from an early age. He started his first business, a letter-writing service for college applicants, while still a teenager. He briefly attended the University of Texas at Austin before dropping out and moving to Los Angeles in the early 1960s, where he intended to enter the entertainment business. He arrived with little money but enormous ambition.

## Asylum Records and the Music Industry

Geffen worked as an agent at the William Morris Agency before founding Asylum Records in 1971. He signed some of the most important musicians of the era, including Jackson Browne, Linda Ronstadt, and the Eagles. Asylum Records became synonymous with California country-rock and the emerging singer-songwriter movement. Geffen developed a reputation for identifying talent before others did and for treating artists with a respect that was unusual in the industry. He sold Asylum to Warner Bros. for $100 million in 1978, making his first fortune.

## Geffen Records and Artist Development

Geffen founded Geffen Records in 1980, one of the most successful independent record labels of the decade. He signed Guns N' Roses, who became the biggest rock act of the late 1980s. He also signed Elton John, Bob Dylan (whose comeback album "Desire" was on Geffen), and Nirvana. The label's success was built on Geffen's personal relationships with artists and his willingness to invest heavily in their development. He was known for giving artists creative freedom that major labels typically withheld.

## DreamWorks and Hollywood

In 1994, Geffen co-founded DreamWorks SKG with Steven Spielberg and Jeffrey Katzenberg. The studio was conceived as a new kind of Hollywood entity — one that combined film, television, and music under one roof. DreamWorks launched with ambitious plans and quickly produced hit films including "American Beauty" and "Saving Private Ryan." Geffen oversaw DreamWorks' music division and later its animation studio, which produced "Shrek," "Kung Fu Panda," and "Madagascar." DreamWorksAnimation was sold to NBCUniversal for $3.8 billion in 2016.

## Cancer Survivor and Health Challenges

Geffen was diagnosed with cancer in the early 2000s and underwent treatment that included surgery and chemotherapy. He survived the illness and emerged as an advocate for cancer research and early detection. His personal battle with the disease gave him a new perspective on his career and legacy. He slowed his pace of work, took fewer deals, and became more selective about his projects. He credited his recovery to the best medical care and to a shift in his approach to stress and work-life balance.

## Art Collection and Philanthropy

Geffen built one of the world's most valuable art collections, estimated to be worth over $2 billion. His collection included works by Mark Rothko, Willem de Kooning, Jackson Pollock, and Ed Ruscha. He donated extensively to UCLA, where the David Geffen School of Medicine bore his name following a $100 million gift. He gave to the Museum of Modern Art and other cultural institutions. ## Net Worth and Recognition

Geffen's net worth was estimated at over $9 billion, making him one of the wealthiest people in entertainment. He owned extensive real estate, including properties in Beverly Hills, Manhattan, and the Hamptons. He sold his catalog of music rights for hundreds of millions of dollars. He was recognized with numerous lifetime achievement awards, including the ASCAP's Golden Note Award and the President's Merit Award from the Recording Industry Association of America. He was a member of the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame.

## Legacy and Influence

Geffen was widely regarded as one of the most influential figures in entertainment history. He transformed the record label business, proved that artists could succeed without sacrificing creative control, and helped build a major Hollywood studio from scratch. He was the subject of the book "The Gefl," a biography that detailed his rise from nothing to billionaire status. His career was a testament to the power of artistic vision combined with business acumen and relentless personal drive.
