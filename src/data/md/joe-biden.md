# Joe Biden — 46th President of the United States

## 1. THE HOOK
On January 20, 2021, Joseph Robinette Biden Jr. stood on the steps of the U.S. Capitol, his hand on his late son Beau's Bible, and became the oldest president in American history at age 78. After decades in politics, he had finally reached the pinnacle of American power — but not before enduring tragedies, failures, and setbacks that would have ended any ordinary career. He ran for president three times before winning, survived a brain aneurysm in 1988, lost his first wife and infant daughter in a car accident in 1972, and buried his son Beau to cancer in 2015. His story is a testament to perseverance through unimaginable personal loss.

## 2. ORIGIN — THE "Before" (The Relatability Engine)
Joe Biden was born on November 20, 1942, in Scranton, Pennsylvania, the first of four children in a working-class Irish Catholic family. His father, James Biden, struggled to find work and the family relied on public assistance during Biden's childhood. Young Joe delivered lunches to earn money and learned early about economic hardship and the dignity of labor. He graduated third in his class from the University of Delaware in 1965, earning a bachelor's in history and a minor in political science, then attended Syracuse University College of Law, graduating 76th out of 85 students in 1968.

After law school, Biden worked as a public defender in Wilmington, Delaware, before entering politics. At age 29, he won his first U.S. Senate race in 1972, defeating incumbent Republican Senator Cale Bogart in an upset that made him one of the youngest senators in American history. Six weeks after his victory, tragedy struck: his wife Neilia and baby daughter Naomi were killed in a car crash while Christmas shopping, and his sons Beau and Hunter were seriously injured. Biden considered resigning to care for his children but was convinced by Senate Majority Leader Mike Mansfield to stay in office, beginning a half-century career that would reshape American politics.

## 3. THE FIRE — Failures, Rock-Bottom & Turning Points
In 1988, Biden's presidential ambitions collapsed when he was caught plagiarizing speeches from British Labour Party leader Neil Kinnock during his first presidential campaign. He also faced questions about his law school grades and his truthfulness. The scandal forced him to withdraw from the race and became a defining humiliation that haunted him for decades. That same year, he suffered a life-threatening brain aneurysm that required emergency surgery. He spent months recovering and relearning basic skills, facing his own mortality for the second time in his life.

His son Beau's death from brain cancer in 2015 became the pivotal turning point of his career. Biden had been preparing for a likely 2016 presidential run but chose not to announce it, citing his grief and the time needed with family. In his eulogy at Beau's funeral, he described his son as a warrior who never gave up and who believed in redemption. The loss deepened Biden's personal faith and resolve, eventually leading him back to the 2020 race with a renewed sense of purpose centered on restoring national unity and honoring his son's memory.

## 4. THE GRIND — The Invisible Years (The Method)
Biden spent 36 years in the U.S. Senate (1973-2009), developing a reputation as a working-class politician who understood blue-collar struggles. He chaired the Senate Judiciary Committee from 1987-1995, where he oversaw the contentious Supreme Court confirmation hearings for Robert Bork and Clarence Thomas. He was instrumental in crafting the Violent Crime Control and Law Enforcement Act of 1994, which created the Office of Community Oriented Policing Services and expanded DNA testing in criminal cases. Biden also championed the Trafficking Victims Protection Act of 2000 and was a leading voice on foreign policy, serving as chairman of the Senate Foreign Relations Committee.

As Vice President under Barack Obama (2009-2017), Biden became the closest adviser to the president, often serving as the bad cop in negotiations with Congress. He managed the stimulus implementation during the 2009 financial crisis and led the administration’s lobbying effort for the 2010 Affordable Care Act. Obama later called Biden "the best vice president in American history." After leaving office, Biden remained active, teaching at the University of Pennsylvania and building his political network. He kept his name in the national conversation through media appearances and speeches, never fully retreating from politics despite his public statements about focusing on family.

## 5. THE TEST — THE BREAKTHROUGH MOMENT
The 2020 presidential election became the ultimate test of Biden's resilience. Facing Donald Trump in the midst of a pandemic, economic collapse, and nationwide protests over racial injustice, Biden ran a low-profile campaign due to COVID-19 restrictions while Trump held large rallies. Biden won 306 electoral votes to Trump's 232, the largest margin for a challenger since 1988, and received over 81 million votes, the most in presidential election history at that time.

The transition was complicated by Trump's refusal to concede and the January 6, 2021 Capitol riot. Biden's inauguration proceeded with unprecedented security following the attack, and he took the oath of office with his hand on the Bible his mother had given him. His inaugural address centered on a call to unity: "We must end this uncivil war." Within weeks, he pursued an aggressive agenda — signing the American Rescue Plan, distributing vaccines, and beginning infrastructure negotiations that would eventually become the Inflation Reduction Act and Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act.

## 7. THE LEGACY — Purpose Beyond Profit + Human Side
Joe Biden's legacy will be defined by his unprecedented perseverance through personal tragedy and political setbacks. His presidency delivered the largest economic stimulus in American history, ended America's longest war in Afghanistan, and restored international alliances after their strain under Trump. He passed legislation on climate change, semiconductor manufacturing, and veterans' benefits. His "Cancer Moonshot" initiative, launched after Beau's death, aimed to halve the cancer death rate within 25 years.

The human side of Biden is revealed in his relationship with his family. He married Jill Jacobs in 1977 and raised stepson Hunter, who struggled with addiction, and Beau, who died at age 46. His granddaughter FinCEN was named after the government bureau Beau had led. Biden continues to receive letters from thousands of Americans sharing their struggles, and he personally responds to many, a habit his staff calls "letter writing" that dates back to his Senate office. He is known for physical warmth with colleagues — hand-holding, shoulder-rubbing, and an inability to stay silent when someone is hurting.
