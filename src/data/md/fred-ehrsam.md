# Fred Ehrsam

**Cofounder of Coinbase, Founder of Paradigm Crypto Fund**

---

## Section 1: From Princeton to Goldman Sachs

Fred Ehrsam's career reads like a study in finding one's true calling after initial success in an unrelated field. As a Princeton-educated computer scientist who chose to work at Goldman Sachs trading bonds rather than writing code, he discovered that the intersection of technology and finance was where his talents lay. His time at Goldman gave him a ringside seat to the dysfunction of traditional financial institutions—their slow settlement times, their insider culture, their resistance to change—and planted the seed for a future in which software would eat finance. He watched as banks spent billions maintaining systems that dated to the 1970s, as traders exploited information asymmetries that only insiders could navigate, and as the broader financial infrastructure creaked under its own weight.

The education was valuable but left him unsatisfied. He knew something better was possible, and he was searching for the problem that would let him build it. Goldman paid well and was prestigious, but it was not a place for someone who wanted to change the world rather than extract value from the existing system. The question was not whether to leave but when, and what to do next. The answer came in the form of a technology that most people in finance had dismissed as a curiosity: Bitcoin.

## Section 2: Bitcoin and the Early Crypto Frontier

Ehrsam left Goldman in 2010, seeing the newly emerging Bitcoin protocol as a technology capable of solving precisely the problems he had observed. Bitcoin's promise was radical: a digital currency that required no trusted intermediary, no banks, no government backing. Transactions could be settled directly between parties, anywhere in the world, at any time, for fees a fraction of what wire transfers cost. The implications for financial inclusion, for cross-border payments, for the entire infrastructure of finance were profound—if the technology worked at scale.

He bought Bitcoin at around $30, one of the earliest institutional investors in the cryptocurrency space, and began building the infrastructure he believed the ecosystem needed. He recognized that the primary barrier to mainstream adoption was friction: buying Bitcoin was complicated, storing it securely was technically challenging, and converting it back to dollars required navigating unfamiliar platforms with poor user experience. The solution was a consumer-friendly exchange that could serve as an on-ramp for millions of users who wanted to participate in the crypto economy but lacked the technical sophistication to manage private keys and blockchain interactions. This insight would lead directly to Coinbase.

## Section 3: Coinbase and the Consumer On-Ramp

His co-founding of Coinbase in 2012 alongside Brian Armstrong represented the first mainstream attempt to build a consumer-friendly Bitcoin exchange in the United States. The company faced enormous challenges: regulatory uncertainty about whether cryptocurrencies were securities or commodities, banking challenges as payment processors refused to work with crypto companies, and skepticism from traditional finance that viewed Bitcoin as a tool for criminals and speculators. The obstacles were not merely technical but systemic—every institution that a consumer-facing company needed to function was hostile or indifferent to crypto.

Ehrsam served as the product lead for the first two years, designing the interface and building the systems that allowed ordinary people to buy and sell cryptocurrency with the ease of buying stocks online. The design philosophy was accessibility: if buying crypto was as easy as buying a stock, millions of people who had been excluded from the asset class would be able to participate. The company persisted through multiple crises—including the failure of its primary banking partner and the Mt. Gox collapse that shook confidence in the entire industry—and eventually became the dominant platform for retail crypto trading in the United States. The persistence was not merely stubbornness: it reflected a genuine conviction that the long-term potential of the technology was worth the short-term pain.

## Section 4: Founding Paradigm

Ehrsam left Coinbase to focus on his own investment work, founding the cryptocurrency trading firm Paradigm in 2018 with Matt Huang, a former Sequoia partner who shared his vision for the industry's long-term potential. Paradigm became one of the most influential crypto-focused investment firms in the industry, backing early investments in Ethereum, Uniswap, and dozens of other protocols that became foundational infrastructure for the crypto economy. The firm's approach was different from traditional venture capital: it invested across the liquidity spectrum from seed rounds to token purchases to market making, reflecting the view that the boundaries between investor and operator were blurring in the crypto world.

Paradigm's portfolio companies included some of the most transformative projects in the space, and the firm's returns reflected the outsized growth of the crypto ecosystem during the bull markets of 2019 through 2021. The firm also participated in the downturn of 2022, when token prices collapsed, exchanges failed, and the industry faced its most serious credibility crisis since Mt. Gox. Ehrsam remained constructive, arguing that the underlying protocols had not been compromised and that the market's excesses had been washed out, leaving a stronger foundation for future growth. The conviction was tested but not broken.

## Section 5: Market Structure and Technical Depth

What distinguishes Ehrsam from many in the cryptocurrency space is his sophisticated understanding of market structure. He has written extensively about the mechanics of crypto markets—liquidity, order books, market making, and the interplay between decentralized protocols and centralized intermediaries—and his writing demonstrates a rare combination of technical depth and strategic vision. He understands not just what crypto can do but how markets actually work: where liquidity hides, how prices get discovered, why certain protocols succeed and others fail.

He argues that the crypto ecosystem is still in its early innings, with the vast majority of financial activity yet to be disrupted, and that the industry's long-term value creation will come from products that actually serve user needs rather than speculative trading. This view leads him to invest in infrastructure—exchanges, protocols, developer tools—rather than in applications that require specific market conditions to succeed. The focus on infrastructure is characteristic of someone who thinks about ecosystems rather than individual companies: the applications will be more visible, but the infrastructure is what enables them.

## Section 6: Ethereum and the Open Platform Vision

Ehrsam has been an advocate for Ethereum, calling it "the most interesting technological development in the world" in one notable interview. The statement was striking coming from an investor who had made his name in Bitcoin: Ethereum represented a different vision of what blockchain technology could accomplish, one based not on a single digital currency but on a platform for building decentralized applications. The vision was more ambitious and more uncertain than Bitcoin's, and it attracted a different kind of developer—ones interested in building applications rather than currencies.

His investments through Paradigm in Ethereum-based protocols reflected this conviction: Uniswap, which became the dominant decentralized exchange; Layer 2 protocols that scale Ethereum's capacity; and tools that make it easier for developers to build on the platform. The investments were not without risk: Ethereum had competed with dozens of other "Ethereum killers" that promised to outperform it and failed. But Ehrsam's analysis suggested that network effects in blockchain platforms were stronger than they appeared, and that the first-mover advantage of Ethereum would prove durable.

## Section 7: Personal Life and Ongoing Influence

Ehrsam lives in San Francisco with his wife and maintains a relatively low public profile despite his influence. He frequently engages with the crypto research community and has been an advocate for the view that cryptocurrency is a fundamental infrastructure improvement for the financial system, not merely a speculative asset class. The distinction matters: if crypto is infrastructure, then its value comes from the applications built on top of it, and investing in infrastructure means investing in the long-term success of the ecosystem rather than in short-term price movements.

His personal investing philosophy reflects this long-term orientation. He is known for patient capital that stays invested through market cycles rather than rotating in and out based on short-term price signals. The approach requires conviction in the technology's long-term potential, and Ehrsam has maintained that conviction through multiple crypto winters, each of which wiped out speculators who had been attracted by the previous bull markets. The persistence is both a reflection of his belief in the technology and a practical outcome of his investment horizon being longer than most institutional capital.

## Section 8: Legacy and Future Directions

Ehrsam's legacy in crypto will likely be defined by the infrastructure investments that enabled the ecosystem to scale. Coinbase became the on-ramp for millions of retail investors; Paradigm became one of the most influential investors in the space; his advocacy for Ethereum helped legitimize a platform that became the foundation for much of the industry's innovation. The contributions are different from those of builders who create visible applications, but they may prove more durable: the infrastructure outlives the applications built on top of it.

The future directions of his work are likely to follow the evolution of the crypto ecosystem itself. As blockchain technology moves from speculation to real utility, as decentralized applications prove their value in contexts beyond trading, the infrastructure that supports them will become more valuable. Ehrsam is well positioned to continue investing in that infrastructure as the industry matures. The crypto winter of 2022, which tested many of the industry's assumptions, also validated the approach of investors who had focused on fundamentals rather than hype. Paradigm survived the downturn with its conviction intact, ready to invest when the market recovered.
