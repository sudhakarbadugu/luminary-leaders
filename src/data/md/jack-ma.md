# Jack Ma — Alibaba Founder

## 1. THE HOOK
He built the world's largest e-commerce company from a modest apartment in Hangzhou, revolutionizing how 1.4 billion Chinese people shop and how global trade flows. Starting from repeated university application failures, he became Asia's richest person and one of the world's most influential entrepreneurs, claiming that "today is difficult, tomorrow is much more difficult, but the day after tomorrow is beautiful."

## 2. ORIGIN — THE "BEFORE" (The Relatability Engine)
Jack Ma (Ma Yun) was born on September 10, 1964, in Hangzhou, Zhejiang Province, China. His parents were traditional musicians—not wealthy, not connected, just ordinary people trying to get by. Young Jack failed his university entrance exam twice, scoring poorly on math, a subject that would later generate his fortune through internet commerce platforms. After eventually passing, he studied English at Hangzhou Normal University, graduating in 1988. He applied for 30 different jobs after graduation, including ten at KFC, and got rejected by every single one. He later recalled how those rejections built resilience that would later serve him in business. During his early career, he worked as an English teacher, earning a modest salary that reflected China's economic conditions in the 1980s.

## 3. THE FIRE — Failures, Rock-Bottom & Turning Points
Ma faced humiliations that would break most aspiring entrepreneurs. In 1995, he traveled to the United States as part of a Chinese trade delegation, where he was exposed to the internet for the first time. He searched for "beer" and "museum" but found no Chinese results—this became his lightbulb moment, but implementing it required overcoming profound skepticism. When he tried to raise funding for Alibaba in 1999, he was rejected by dozens of venture capitalists who thought China couldn't support online commerce. He famously endured a meeting with孙正义(SoftBank's Masayoshi Son) where he pitched for 5 minutes before being interrupted, yet walked away with $20 million anyway. His early employees worked out of his apartment, surviving on minimal salaries, with many believing they were chasing an impossible dream.

## 4. THE GRIND — The Invisible Years (The Method)
Before Alibaba's breakthrough, Ma spent nearly a decade laying foundations. From 1988 to 1999, he worked as an English teacher while moonlighting on various projects—starting a translation business, working for a government trade mission, and taking his first trip to the US where he discovered the internet. He traveled to the US in 1995 as part of a trade delegation and spent months studying how American businesses operated. During this period, he visited dozens of companies, absorbing their approaches to customer service, logistics, and technology adoption. He made numerous friends in the American technology industry who would later advise him on building Alibaba. This patient observation and relationship-building phase equipped him with the business instincts that would prove crucial when he finally launched his own venture.

## 5. THE TEST — THE BREAKTHROUGH MOMENT
On April 4, 1999, Jack Ma gathered 17 people in his apartment and formally founded Alibaba, betting that Chinese manufacturers could reach global buyers through the internet. The timing was perfect—the Chinese economy was beginning its explosive growth phase, and the country had virtually no business-to-business e-commerce infrastructure. Within three years, Alibaba had 4 million users and was profitable. In 2003, Taobao was launched to compete with eBay, and by 2005, it had overtaken eBay in China through a strategy of no listing fees and free services—a approach that eBay's American executives initially dismissed as unsustainable. Ma's boldness reached its peak in 2005 when Alibaba received a $1 billion investment from SoftBank and Yahoo, validating his vision and providing war chest for global expansion.

## 7. THE LEGACY — Purpose Beyond Profit + Human Side
Jack Ma's Alibaba created over 10 million jobs globally and transformed Chinese entrepreneurship by showing that Chinese companies could compete technologically with American giants. Through his personal foundation, he has committed billions to education, environmental conservation, and healthcare—particularly in rural China where poverty remains entrenched. He established the Jack Ma Foundation, which funds distance learning for teachers in poor regions, scholarships for students from rural areas, and environmental programs including the Yunnan Nature Fund. His openness about his failures—including how a 1999 meeting with a venture capitalist ended badly—made him unusually relatable to aspiring entrepreneurs across Asia. After stepping back from Alibaba in 2019 following regulatory scrutiny, he has focused on promoting arts education and supporting African entrepreneurs through the Africa Netrepreneur Prize.
