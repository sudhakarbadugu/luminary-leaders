# Dr. Lisa Su

**CEO, Advanced Micro Devices (AMD) | Engineered the Greatest Corporate Turnaround in Tech History**

Dr. Lisa Su is the Taiwanese-American semiconductor executive who transformed AMD from a struggling also-ran into a major force in high-performance computing. Her technical expertise combined with strategic vision and execution excellence resulted in one of the most remarkable business recoveries in corporate history, creating over $50 billion in shareholder value during her tenure.

## Background and Education

Lisa Tzwu-Fang Su was born in 1969 in Taiwan and immigrated to the United States as a child, settling in Houston, Texas. She earned her Bachelor's in Electrical Engineering from the National Taiwan University at age seventeen, then completed her PhD in Electrical Engineering from the California Institute of Technology (Caltech) in 1994. Her doctoral research focused on semiconductor device modeling and characterization—deep technical expertise that would later inform her product strategy and customer relationships. She began her career at Texas Instruments and later worked at IBM and Freescale Semiconductor before joining AMD in 2012.

## Joining AMD and Early Challenges

Su joined AMD in January 2012 as Senior Vice President and General Manager of the global business units, initially overseeing the company's embedded and semi-custom businesses. These divisions, including the successful Semi-Custom Solutions group that designed chips for gaming consoles, provided stable revenue while she assessed the company's broader challenges. When she became CEO in October 2014, AMD was losing money, its processor designs were falling behind Intel, and the company's market capitalization had stagnated for years. Wall Street considered AMD a declining also-ran in a duopoly market.

## Zen Architecture and Product Renaissance

Su's strategic insight was to bet everything on a completely new processor architecture. She redirected AMD's engineering resources toward developing "Zen," a ground-up redesign of AMD's CPU architecture that would deliver dramatic improvements in instructions per clock cycle. The 2017 launch of the Ryzen processor line demonstrated that AMD had closed the performance gap with Intel, offering competitive products at significantly lower price points. This success continued with the Zen 2 architecture in 2019 and Zen 3 in 2020, each generation extending AMD's competitive advantages. Meanwhile, AMD's server market share grew from essentially zero to over 20% within five years.

## EPYC and Data Center Dominance

The launch of the AMD EPYC server processor line in 2017 marked the company's return to the data center market it had abandoned years earlier. Cloud providers like Amazon Web Services, Microsoft Azure, and Google Cloud rapidly adopted AMD processors for their superior price-performance ratios. By the late 2010s, AMD was winning contracts previously considered Intel-exclusive, and server processor demand was growing faster than any other segment. The data center market became AMD's largest revenue driver and a symbol of the company's technical credibility restored.

## Graphics and Gaming Leadership

Su also oversaw the transformation of AMD's graphics business. The launch of the Radeon RX 480 in 2016 signaled AMD's return to the consumer GPU market, delivering strong performance at accessible price points. AMD's "Navi" architecture in 2019 further improved its competitive position, and the company secured major design wins in gaming consoles, powering PlayStation 5, Xbox Series X, and Xbox Series S. This semi-custom revenue stream provided stable cash flow during the processor turnaround, demonstrating Su's ability to balance long-term strategic transformation with short-term financial stability.

## Leadership Philosophy and Execution

Su is known for her calm, methodical leadership style—a contrast to the aggressive bravado typical of tech executives. She emphasizes execution over vision, stating that great strategies fail without flawless implementation. She maintains close relationships with major customers and personally oversees key partnerships. Her ability to align engineering priorities with market opportunities, manage capital allocation across long-horizon R&D programs, and maintain employee morale during difficult periods characterizes her leadership approach. Under her tenure, AMD's market capitalization grew from under $3 billion in 2014 to over $50 billion, making it one of the best-performing tech stocks of the decade.

## Recognition and Impact

Su has been widely recognized for her achievements, appearing on Fortune's World's Most Powerful Women list and receiving the IEEE CEO of the Year award. She serves on the boards of Cisco and the Global Semiconductor Alliance. Her turnaround of AMD is studied in business schools as a case study in strategic patience, technical excellence, and organizational transformation. She demonstrates that deep technical expertise and strategic leadership can coexist in modern technology companies, offering a template for future engineering leaders who aim to climb from technical contributor to executive decision-maker.
