# John Neff — The Professional's Professional Who Beat the S&P for 31 of 35 Years

## 1. THE HOOK

He managed Vanguard Windsor Fund from 1964 to 1995. He beat the S&P 500 for 31 of 35 years. He returned approximately 13.7% annualized versus the market's 10%. He turned $1 into $22. He was called the "professional's professional." He never appeared on magazine covers. He avoided hot stocks. He bought unloved sectors. He held for 3-5 years. His philosophy: "Value investing with a twist." He is John Neff.

How does a low-profile investor beat the S&P 500 for 31 of 35 years?

## 2. ORIGIN — THE "Before" (The Relatability Engine)

John Neff was born in 1931. He joined Wellington Management Company. He took over Vanguard Windsor Fund in 1964.

He was running from hot stocks. He did not chase popular growth stories. He invested in what was out of favor.

## 3. THE FIRE — Failures, Rock-Bottom & Turning Points

### Vanguard Windsor (1964-1995)

Neff managed Vanguard Windsor Fund for 31 years. He beat the S&P 500 for 31 of 35 years. He returned approximately 13.7% annualized.

### Performance

$1 invested in Windsor became $22. The S&P 500 turned $1 into approximately $10.

### Low Profile

Neff never appeared on magazine covers. He was called the "professional's professional." He let his results speak.

### Book

Neff wrote "John Neff on Investing" (1999). He shared his methods.

## 4. THE GRIND — The Invisible Years (The Method)

Neff's method is defined by P/E discipline, dividend yield, and contrarian positioning.

### P/E Discipline

Neff bought stocks at a 40-50% discount to the market's P/E. He did not overpay.

### Dividend Yield Focus

Neff favored stocks yielding 4-5% or higher. Dividends provided income and margin of safety.

### Contrarian Positioning

Neff invested in sectors that were disliked. Energy. Financials. Utilities. He bought when others sold.

### Patience

Neff held positions for 3-5 years. He waited for recognition.

The invisible years—the years of beating the S&P—were years of discipline. Neff did not chase hot stocks.

## 5. THE TEST — The Breakthrough Moment

The track record—beating the S&P for 31 of 35 years—is the test. Neff was consistent.
