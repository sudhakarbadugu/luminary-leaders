# Stewart Butterfield — The Philosopher Who Pivoted Twice to Billions

## 1. THE HOOK

In 2002, he built a game called Game Neverending. It failed. He pivoted to Flickr. Yahoo bought it for $35 million. In 2009, he built a game called Glitch. It failed. He pivoted to Slack. Salesforce bought it for $27.7 billion. Two games failed. Two pivots. Two mega-exits. He says: "It's really hard to design products by focus groups. A lot of times, people don't know what they want until you show it to them."

How does a philosophy major from Manitoba turn two failed games into billions?

## 2. ORIGIN — THE "Before" (The Relatability Engine)

Stewart Butterfield was born March 21, 1973, in Morris, Manitoba, Canada. He studied at University of Victoria (BA philosophy), Cambridge (MPhil philosophy).

He was running from traditional careers. He saw games. He wanted to build social experiences.

## 3. THE FIRE — Failures, Rock-Bottom & Turning Points

### Ludicorp and Flickr (2002-2005)

In 2002, Butterfield founded Ludicorp. He built Game Neverending. It failed. The team pivoted to a photo-sharing tool they built internally. It became Flickr.

### Flickr Exit (2005)

In 2005, Yahoo acquired Flickr for ~$35 million.

### Tiny Speck and Slack (2009-2021)

In 2009, Butterfield founded Tiny Speck. He built Glitch. In 2012, Glitch was shut down. The team recognized the internal communication tool they built was valuable.

### Slack (2013)

In 2013, Slack launched publicly. Enterprise messaging. Channels. Integrations. Search.

### Slack IPO (2019)

In 2019, Slack went public via direct listing.

### Salesforce Acquisition (2021)

In 2021, Salesforce acquired Slack for $27.7 billion. One of the largest software acquisitions ever.

## 4. THE GRIND — The Invisible Years (The Method)

Butterfield's method is defined by game-first pivot to product, philosophy-driven decisions, and authentic culture.

### Game-First, Pivot to Product

Butterfield's pattern: Build a game. If it fails, pivot to the internal tool. Flickr and Slack both started as games.

### Philosophy-Driven Decisions

Butterfield's philosophy background shapes his approach. Deep thinking about problems.

### Quirky Culture

Butterfield maintained a "History of the World" internal Slack channel. Authentic culture-building.

The invisible years—the years of failed games—taught Butterfield to recognize hidden value. He pivoted twice.

## 5. THE TEST — THE BREAKTHROUGH MOMENT

$27.7 billion is the test. Slack became essential infrastructure. Pivots work.

## 6. THE PHILOSOPHY — Stealable Wisdom

## 7. THE LEGACY — Purpose Beyond Profit + Human Side

Stewart Butterfield left Slack after the Salesforce acquisition. He invests. He builds.

### What does he want to be remembered for?

In interviews, Butterfield emphasizes authentic culture and product intuition. He wants to reduce friction in work.
