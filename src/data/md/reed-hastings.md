# Reed Hastings — The Culture Innovator Who Disrupted Entertainment

## 1. THE HOOK

In 1997, he cofounded Netflix with Marc Randolph. They started with DVD rentals by mail. In 2000, they tried to sell Netflix to Blockbuster for $50 million. Blockbuster rejected them. In 2007, Netflix pivoted to streaming. In 2013, they launched original content with House of Cards. Netflix destroyed Blockbuster. His culture deck went viral. He says: "Adequate performance gets a generous severance."

How does a math major from Boston destroy Blockbuster?

## 2. ORIGIN — THE "Before" (The Relatability Engine)

Reed Hastings was born October 8, 1960, in Boston, Massachusetts. He studied at Bowdoin College (BA mathematics), Stanford (MS computer science). He founded Pure Software in 1991. He sold it to Rational Software in 1996 for $750 million.

He was running from traditional media. He saw subscription models. He wanted to disrupt entertainment.

## 3. THE FIRE — Failures, Rock-Bottom & Turning Points

### Pure Software (1991-1996)

Hastings founded Pure Software. Debugging tools. Sold to Rational Software for $750 million. Exit capital.

### Netflix (1997-Present)

In 1997, Hastings cofounded Netflix with Marc Randolph. DVD rentals by mail.

### Blockbuster Rejection (2000)

In 2000, Netflix tried to sell to Blockbuster for $50 million. Blockbuster rejected them. Hastings kept going.

### Streaming Pivot (2007)

In 2007, Netflix pivoted from DVDs to streaming. Hastings cannibalized his own business model.

### Original Content (2013)

In 2013, Netflix launched House of Cards. Original content. Binge-watch model. Stranger Things, Squid Game followed.

### Culture Deck (2009)

Hastings' "Culture Deck" went viral. Freedom and Responsibility. No vacation policy. Talent density. Keepers test.

### Step Down as CEO (2023)

In 2023, Hastings stepped down as CEO. Ted Sarandos and Greg Peters became co-CEOs. Hastings is executive chairman.

## 4. THE GRIND — The Invisible Years (The Method)

Hastings' method is defined by talent density, freedom and responsibility, and no brilliant jerks.

### Talent Density

Hastings' philosophy: Hire fewer but better people. Pay top of market. Mediocrity removed.

### Freedom and Responsibility

Hastings' philosophy: No vacation policy. No expense approvals. Freedom paired with high accountability.

### No Brilliant Jerks

Hastings' philosophy: "Do not tolerate brilliant jerks." Cultural fit matters more than raw talent.

The invisible years—the years of culture innovation—gave Hastings management mastery. He wrote "No Rules Rules" with Erin Meyer.

## 5. THE TEST — The Breakthrough Moment

Streaming is the test. Netflix pivoted from DVDs. Original content. Blockbuster destroyed. Culture deck viral.
