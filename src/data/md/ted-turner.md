# Ted Turner — The Man Who Invented 24-Hour News

## 1. THE HOOK

Ted Turner founded CNN in 1980—the world's first 24-hour news channel. He launched Headline News, WTBS Superstation, and the Turner Broadcasting System. He owned the Atlanta Braves (MLB), Atlanta Hawks (NBA), and founded the Goodwill Games. He once said: "You can never give up. When you're too proud to quit, that's when things start happening."

How did a son of a billboards tycoon build a media empire that changed how news is consumed globally?

## 2. ORIGIN — THE "BEFORE" (The Relatability Engine)

Ted Turner was born November 19, 1938, in Cincinnati, Ohio, to a wealthy family. His father, Fred Turner, owned Turner Advertising Company—a billboard company that covered the southeastern United States. The family lived in a large house with servants.

Young Ted was rebellious. He attended Brown University but was expelled in his sophomore year for filming a nude woman on campus (he claimed it was a prank). His father sent him to work at the family business over the next two years. He later returned to Brown and graduated in 1960 with a degree in classics.

After graduation, Turner joined the family billboard business. He took over the struggling Memphis division and turned it profitable within two years. His father noticed his sales talent and promoted him to run the entire Southeast region. He learned to sell advertising space—the core skill that would later define his media empire.

In 1963, his father died by suicide. Ted was 24. He inherited Turner Advertising Company with $800,000 in debt. He took over with a chip on his shoulder. He later said: "I decided I wouldn't let Dad's death destroy me. I would make the company succeed."

## 3. THE FIRE — FAILURES, ROCK-BOTTOM & TURNING POINTS

### The Discotheque Failure (1970s)

Turner tried to enter nightclub business in Atlanta. He opened a discotheque called "The Great Gatsby" in 1970. It failed within two years. He lost $200,000—real money at the time. He was embarrassed.

He reframed his approach: "I failed because I didn't understand the customer. I thought fancy decorations would attract people. I was wrong." The failure taught him to focus on content over aesthetics.

### Buying WJRG (1970)

In 1970, Turner bought a failing UHF station in Atlanta (WJRG) for $40,000. He renamed it WTBS. He broadcast Atlanta Braves baseball games and old movies. The station had weak signal and limited audience. Critics laughed.

Turner thought differently: "Cable TV was growing. If I could get on satellite, I could broadcast anywhere." He invested $25,000 to build a small satellite dish at the station—an enormous risk at the time.

### Satellite Broadcast Breakthrough (1976)

In 1976, Turner leased satellite transponder capacity for $500,000 annually—half his company's revenue. He broadcast WTBS to cable systems nationwide. The first satellite broadcast was December 27, 1976—covering a college football bowl game. Cable operators signed up because they needed content.

By 1978, WTBS had 3 million subscribers. Turner called it "the first superstation"—a local broadcast channel with national reach. Advertisers paid premium rates to reach the audience.

### CNN Launch (1980)

In 1980, Turner launched CNN (Cable News Network). He hired 300 employees. He broadcast 24 hours per day, 7 days per week. Critics called it "Chicken Noodle News" and "the turner broadcasting garbage."

The first broadcast was June 1, 1980. Audience was tiny (150,000 households). The network lost $30 million in its first year. Turner's board threatened to shut it down. Turner refused: "Give me two years. By 1982, we'll be profitable."

He was right. CNN became the primary news source for hotels, airports, and homes without cable. The Iran hostage crisis (1979-1981) made 24-hour news essential. CNN's coverage of the Gulf War (1991) cemented its authority.

### The Failed Merger with CBS (1985)

In 1985, Turner made a hostile bid for CBS. He offered $3.5 billion. CBS management rejected it. They called Turner "an eccentric cowpoke" who couldn't run a major network. Turner lost the bid and spent $50 million in legal fees.

He was humiliated. He told Time magazine: "I wanted to prove I belonged with the big boys. Instead, I learned that belonging isn't everything."

### The Goodwill Games (1986)

Turner created the Goodwill Games in 1986 as an alternative to the Olympics (which were boycotted by the US and USSR). He spent $50 million on the event. It was broadcast on WTBS and CNN. The games lost money but generated immense goodwill (pun intended) and proved Turner's ability to organize global events.

## 4. THE GRIND — THE INVISIBLE YEARS (THE METHOD)

### Content is King (Before Redstone)

Turner's philosophy predated Sumner Redstone's: content was always king. He owned sports teams, news networks, and movie libraries because content attracted audiences that advertisers paid to reach.

### Aggressive Growth

Turner borrowed heavily to fund acquisitions. He believed debt was a tool, not a risk. He once said: "You can't make big decisions with small thinking." He leveraged his superstation cash flow to buy media assets during downturns.

### Risk Tolerance

Turner launched CNN knowing it could fail. He spent half his company's revenue on untested satellite technology. He built an Olympics alternative when governments couldn't agree. He took risks others called insane.

### Vertical Integration

Turner owned content creation (Turner Studios), distribution (satellite network), and audience (cable systems). This vertical integration let him control costs and quality throughout the chain.

## 5. THE TEST — THE BREAKTHROUGH MOMENT

By 1990, Turner Broadcasting had 8,000 employees, $1.5 billion in annual revenue. CNN had 23 international bureaus. The Atlanta Braves had become a winning team (won World Series in 1995). Turner owned the world's largest animation library (hitting 2,000 hours after acquiring Hanna-Barbera in 1991).

In 1996, Time Warner acquired Turner Broadcasting for $7.5 billion. Turner received $7.5 billion in Time Warner stock and became the company's largest shareholder. He served as vice chairman until 2003.

His test: can you build something lasting? CNN survived and thrived. The Goodwill Games continued until 2001. Turner Broadcasting became a foundation of modern cable TV.

## 6. THE PHILOSOPHY — STEALABLE WISDOM

**On Persistence:**
"You can never give up. When you're too proud to quit, that's when things start happening."

**On Risk:**
"You can't make big decisions with small thinking. If you're not willing to lose everything, you'll never gain everything."

**On Content:**
"A news network is like a fire department. You hope nothing happens, but when it does, you better be ready."

## 7. THE LEGACY — PURPOSE BEYOND PROFIT + HUMAN SIDE

Ted Turner donated $1 billion to the United Nations Foundation (2009)—the largest individual donation in UN history. He created the UN Foundation to support UN causes including climate change, conflict resolution, and public health.

He also owns 2 million acres in Montana, Wyoming, and Colorado (one of the largest private landholders in America). He funds sustainable ranching and wildlife conservation.

He was married three times (to Judy Smith, Marge Reif, and Jane Smith). He has five children. He was diagnosed with manic depression in the 1990s. He publicly discussed his mental health to reduce stigma.

He wants to be remembered as someone who built global communication infrastructure and gave back to causes larger than himself.

2. **Create content others need** — CNN filled a gap (24-hour news). What gap exists in your market that you could fill?

3. **Persist when critics laugh** — CNN was called "Chicken Noodle News." Continue when people mock your vision.

4. **Leverage assets aggressively** — Turner borrowed against his superstation to fund growth. Use your existing assets to fund new opportunities.

5. **Give back systematically** — Turner donated $1 billion. Start with 1% of your earnings toward causes you care about.
