# David Rubenstein — The Patriotic Investor Who Built Carlyle

## 1. THE HOOK

He cofounded The Carlyle Group in 1987. Today, Carlyle manages over $400 billion. He served as deputy domestic policy advisor to President Carter. He is known for political connections—ex-presidents and politicians as advisors. He hosts "The David Rubenstein Show" on PBS. He interviews leaders. He signed the Giving Pledge. He donated millions to education and the Smithsonian. He wrote "The American Story" and "How to Lead." His famous quote: "I've always believed that you can make money and do good at the same time."

How does a lawyer become a private equity billionaire with political connections?

## 2. ORIGIN — THE "Before" (The Relatability Engine)

David Rubenstein was born August 11, 1949, in Baltimore, Maryland. He studied at Duke and University of Chicago Law School. He worked at Skadden Arps. He served as deputy domestic policy advisor to President Carter from 1977 to 1981.

He was running from conventional law. He saw an opportunity in private equity. He cofounded Carlyle in 1987.

## 3. THE FIRE — Failures, Rock-Bottom & Turning Points

### The Carlyle Group (1987)

In 1987, Rubenstein cofounded Carlyle with William Conway and Daniel D'Aniello. The firm focuses on defense companies and political connections.

### Political Connections

Carlyle became known for "patriotic duty" deals—defense companies. Ex-presidents and politicians served as advisors. The connections brought deal flow.

### Carlyle Today

Carlyle manages over $400 billion. It is one of the largest private equity firms globally.

### The David Rubenstein Show

Rubenstein hosts "The David Rubenstein Show" on PBS. He interviews leaders. He shares insights.

### Books

Rubenstein wrote "The American Story" (2019) and "How to Lead" (2020).

## 4. THE GRIND — The Invisible Years (The Method)

Rubenstein's method is defined by relationships, political connections, and global reach.

### Relationship-Driven Investing

Rubenstein built a network of global leaders and executives. The relationships bring deal flow.

### Political Connections

Carlyle's political connections—ex-presidents, politicians—provide access and insights. "Access investing."

### Global Reach

Carlyle invests globally. The firm has offices and investments around the world.

The invisible years—the years of building Carlyle—were years of building relationships.

## 5. THE TEST — The Breakthrough Moment

Carlyle's growth—from startup to $400 billion—is the test. Rubenstein built a global firm.

## 6. THE PHILOSOPHY — Stealable Wisdom

**On Money and Good:**
"I've always believed that you can make money and do good at the same time."

**On Philanthropy:**
"Giving away money is much harder than making it."

## 7. THE LEGACY — Purpose Beyond Profit + Human Side

David Rubenstein is still active. He runs Carlyle. He hosts his show. He gives.

### Philanthropy

Rubenstein signed the Giving Pledge. He donated to Duke, Harvard, Johns Hopkins, and the Smithsonian. He funded the African American museum and the panda exhibit at the National Zoo.

### What does he want to be remembered for?

In interviews, Rubenstein emphasizes giving, leadership, and patriotism. He wants investors to make money and do good.
